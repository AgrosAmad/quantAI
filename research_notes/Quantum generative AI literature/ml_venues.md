# Quantum computing and quantum ML papers at major ML venues, 2018 to November 2026

Scope: NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, IJCAI, KDD, UAI, AISTATS, COLT, ACL/EMNLP, JMLR, TMLR, main tracks and co-located workshops. Compiled 2026-11-03.

How the enumeration was done (so the reader knows the coverage limits):
- Official proceedings were scanned programmatically for "quantum", "qubit" and "tensor network" in paper titles: papers.nips.cc year pages (NeurIPS 2018 to 2025), the neurips.cc virtual program for NeurIPS 2026, PMLR volume indices (ICML v80, v97, v119, v139, v162, v202, v235, v267, v306; AISTATS v84, v89, v108, v130, v151, v206, v238, v258, v300; UAI v115, v124, v161, v180, v216, v244, v286, v337; COLT v75, v99, v125, v134, v178, v195, v247, v291, v336), ijcai.org proceedings pages 2018 to 2025, aclanthology.org event pages (ACL and EMNLP 2018 to 2025), jmlr.org volumes 19 to 27, and the static iclr.cc schedule or virtual pages for ICLR 2018 to 2023.
- OpenReview's search API (9,050 notes matching "quantum" in title or abstract) supplied ICLR 2024 to 2026, NeurIPS 2023 to 2025, ICML 2023 to 2026, TMLR, AISTATS/UAI 2025 to 2026 and all NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR workshop papers hosted there; the older API (ICLR 2018 to 2023, NeurIPS 2021 to 2022) was searched the same way. Acceptance for ICLR 2018 to 2021 submissions was checked against the official iclr.cc schedule or virtual pages because the old OpenReview notes carry no decision field.
- AAAI, IJCAI and KDD were enumerated through Crossref DOI records (prefixes 10.1609, 10.24963, 10.1145 with SIGKDD container titles) because ojs.aaai.org and dblp.org sit behind a bot wall; UAI 2018 was read from auai.org.
- arXiv identifiers were resolved through DataCite (arXiv DOI registry, exact title match); journal venues of named candidate papers were verified through Crossref. The arXiv API, Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex were rate-limited during the session, so a paper without an arXiv ID below is one DataCite could not match, not necessarily one without a preprint.
- Title-only scanning misses papers that mention quantum only in the abstract; where OpenReview abstracts were searchable (2022 onward) the abstract-only hits were almost entirely quantum chemistry or "quantum-inspired" wording and are not listed unless noted.
- "Quantum" here means quantum computing, quantum information or quantum machine learning. Quantum chemistry datasets (QM9, QH9, nabla2DFT), "quantum-dot" imaging and purely metaphorical uses are excluded or explicitly flagged as peripheral.

## Key question 1: Which quantum papers were accepted in the main tracks, venue by venue and year by year?

### Takeaway
Quantum computing papers have been a small but steadily growing presence: roughly 2 to 5 per year at NeurIPS from 2018 to 2021, 8 to 14 per year in 2022 to 2025, then 45 quantum-titled accepted papers at NeurIPS 2026; ICML went from 0 to 1 per year (2018 to 2019) to 16 to 23 per year (2023 to 2026); ICLR from 0 to 2 per year before 2023 to 10 to 14 per year in 2025 to 2026. Learning theory venues (COLT, AISTATS, UAI) carry a consistent trickle of quantum learning theory; AAAI has the most quantum titles of the AI venues (about 60 since 2018) but many are "quantum-inspired" NLP; KDD, IJCAI, ACL/EMNLP and JMLR each have only a handful.

## Key question 2: Which of the accepted papers are generative, and where did the well-known quantum generative papers actually appear?

## Key question 3: Which quantum-ML workshops ran at these venues and what generative papers appeared there?

### Takeaway
Until 2026 there was no dedicated quantum machine learning workshop at NeurIPS, ICML or ICLR in the 2018 to 2025 window (the last NeurIPS "Quantum Machine Learning" workshop was 2015; the 2020 "Quantum Tensor Networks in Machine Learning" workshop is the closest); quantum papers instead appear in the NeurIPS Machine Learning and the Physical Sciences (ML4PS) workshop every year, in AI4Science workshops at NeurIPS/ICML, in NeurIPS 2024's Machine Learning with New Compute Paradigms (MLNCP), in ICLR 2023's Physics4ML, and in ICML 2026's AI4Physics. ## Key question 5: Trends, groups and whether generative quantum work is growing at ML venues

### Takeaway
Quantum-titled main-track acceptances at NeurIPS+ICML+ICLR grew from 3 (2018) to about 10 (2021), about 24 (2023), about 34 (2024), about 46 (2025) and about 78 (2026, with NeurIPS 2026 alone at 45); generative quantum papers remain a small fraction (roughly 1 per year until 2025, about 6 in 2026). The most frequent author clusters in the data are Chinese quantum-algorithms groups around Tongyang Li and coauthors (optimization, games, bandits, speedups), the Junchi Yan group at Shanghai Jiao Tong (QAS-Bench, QuantumDARTS, QVAE-Mole, SSL4Q, LLM4QPE, QuaDiM, QEM-Bench, QuanONet, Node2ket), Yuxuan Du with Xinbiao Wang and Kaining Zhang (symmetric pruning, kernel auto-design, AQER, PALQO, AiDE-Q, neural decoders), the Leiden group of Vedran Dunjko (parametrized quantum policies, measure-first limitations, advantages beyond hardness, IQP Born machines), the Berlin group of Jens Eisert and Elies Gil-Fuster (dequantization versus trainability, kernel inference complexity), Eric Anschuetz (quantum generative model and QNN loss landscapes), Srinivasan Arunachalam (quantum boosting, private learning, agnostic boosting, sample complexity), Google Quantum AI authors (quantum backpropagation; communication advantage), IBM authors (contextual optimal transport; doubly stochastic transformers; wavefunction flows), ORCA Computing (quantum latent distributions), QC Ware-affiliated authors Kerenidis, Landman, Prakash and Mathur (q-means, QEM, quantum CNN algorithms, random-Fourier dequantization) and the tensor-network group of Rabusseau, Miller and Terilla (AISTATS, ICML, JMLR).

### Inferences
- Generative quantum work is growing at ML venues, but mostly through (a) quantum priors or latents inside classical generative pipelines (ORCA, QVAE-Mole, quantum-assisted VAEs), (b) theory of Born machines and loss landscapes (Anschuetz; Shen, Dunjko et al.), and (c) classical generative models for quantum data (QuaDiM, BuresTomFlow, flows for quantum dynamics). Quantum diffusion models proper have so far appeared only at workshops (MLNCP 2024, ML4PS 2025) and in physics journals.
- The strongest growth areas are quantum optimization theory (NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR), AI-for-quantum (decoders, architecture search, control) and, since 2024, benchmark and dataset papers (QAS-Bench, QEM-Bench, QCircuitBench, QEC Model Zoo, QuaMap), which suggests the ML community is treating quantum computing increasingly as an application domain for ML rather than as a source of new learning algorithms.
