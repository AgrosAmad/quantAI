## Key question 1: Quantum circuit Born machines (QCBM): foundations, losses, generalisation, trainability, and the 2023 to 2026 follow-ups

### Takeaway
The QCBM was proposed twice in 2018: Benedetti et al. framed "data-driven quantum circuit learning" as a hardware benchmark trained with a clipped-KL cost, and Liu and Wang coined "quantum circuit Born machine" and gave the first gradient-based training via a kernel maximum mean discrepancy (MMD). Between 2019 and 2024 the field explored alternative losses (Sinkhorn and Stein discrepancies, adversarial objectives, f-divergences), rigorous generalisation metrics (Gili et al.), trainability theory (barren plateaus and loss concentration, Rudolph et al.) and complexity-theoretic learnability results (Sweke, Hinsche, Nietner). The dominant 2025 to 2026 direction is "train classically, deploy on quantum" using instantaneous quantum polynomial (IQP) Born machines whose MMD loss is classically computable while sampling remains classically hard; this line now reaches 1000 simulated qubits and 316-qubit IQP circuits executed on IBM Heron hardware, and Google's 68-qubit "generative quantum advantage" experiment (Sept 2025) is the most prominent recent claim.

### Inferences
- The field's centre of gravity moved from "which loss to run on hardware" (2018 to 2022) to "which model classes are classically trainable yet quantumly hard to sample" (2025 to 2026); the IQP-QCBM programme and Google's 2025 experiment are the two places where a credible generative advantage argument now lives.
- The most defensible empirical claims for QCBMs over classical baselines are in the data-limited regime (Gili 2023, Hibat-Allah 2024); none of the hardware papers claims advantage at scale, and several (Zhu 2022 copula follow-up, Rudolph 2024) document degrading trainability with size.

## Key question 2: Quantum Boltzmann machines (QBM), quantum Hamiltonian-based models, and D-Wave Boltzmann-machine generative work

### Takeaway
Amin et al. (arXiv 2016, PRX 2018) defined the QBM as a Gibbs state of a transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian trained on a bound of the log-likelihood; Kieferová and Wiebe (2017) showed how to train it on quantum data (tomography) with relative-entropy objectives. After a quieter period dominated by annealer-based (restricted) Boltzmann machines and the variational QBM of Zoufal et al. (2021), the family was revived from 2023 onward by rigorous results: Coopmans and Benedetti proved sample-efficient trainability of fully visible QBMs (relative entropy is convex, polynomial sample complexity), Patel, Minervini and Wilde developed natural-gradient and "evolved" QBMs, Tüysüz et al. and Demidik et al. clarified expressivity (low-dimensional QBMs generating high-dimensional data; quantum RBMs no more expressive than classical ones), and Wilde (Dec 2025) gave the first general training recipe for generative modelling with evolved QBMs. On annealers, 2025 to 2026 work reaches 2000 qubits on D-Wave Advantage2 for Boltzmann-prior VAEs.

- Theory now points toward fully visible QBMs (convex, sample-efficient) and evolved QBMs as the trainable sub-classes, while restricted QBMs lose both on trainability (non-commuting hidden units) and on expressivity (Demidik 2025).
- Annealer-based generative work is best read as "quantum-assisted sampling for classical energy-based models"; the strongest 2026 claims (Kim and Park) are against a Gaussian-prior VAE, not against a well-tuned classical Boltzmann sampler, so they do not establish advantage.

### Gaps
- I found no gate-model hardware demonstration of a QBM trained for generative modelling at more than a handful of qubits; the Patel, Minervini and Wilde papers are theoretical or simulated as far as their abstracts state.
- No D-Wave corporate paper on QBM generative modelling from 2024 to 2026 surfaced in my searches beyond the academic Advantage2 work; this may reflect search coverage rather than absence.

## Key question 3: Tensor-network (quantum-inspired) generative models and their links to quantum circuits

### Takeaway
Han, Wang, Fan, Wang and Zhang (PRX 2018) introduced the MPS Born machine with exact likelihoods, DMRG-style two-site training and direct sampling; Cheng et al. extended it to tree tensor networks (PRB 2019) and Glasser et al. (NeurIPS 2019) proved separations between MPS, Born machines and locally purified states (LPS) and connected 2-local circuits to LPS. Tensor-network Born machines became the quantum-inspired baseline of choice (Zapata's TN-GEO, symmetric TNs) and the pre-training tool for quantum circuits (Dborin et al. 2022; Rudolph et al., Nature Communications 2023). 2025 to 2026 work concentrates on optimisation (second-order methods, initialisation), adaptive tree structures, sequence modelling with trainable embeddings, and approximation-complexity theory for MPO Born machines.

## Key question 4: Quantum autoregressive, transformer, recurrent and language models (generative uses only)

### Takeaway
Quantum self-attention started with Li, Zhao and Wang's QSANN (2022, published 2024) for text classification; the generative and large-model direction arrived with Cherrat et al.'s quantum vision transformers (Quantum 2024), Guo et al.'s fault-tolerant quantum-linear-algebra transformer (2024), Liao and Ferrie's "GPT on a quantum computer" (2024), Quantinuum's Quixer (2024) and the Chen and Kuo text-generation benchmarks (2025). True quantum autoregressive generative models are rare: Anschuetz et al. proved an interpretable advantage for Born-machine sequence learning (PRX Quantum 2023), and 2026 preprints (CoMB, QFAN) generate samples component by component from a small register with coherent memory. QNLP (lambeq, DisCoCat/DisCoCirc) is mostly classification and question answering; the generative exception is Karamlou et al. (2022). Hybrid quantum LLMs (HyQuT, 150M parameters, 10 qubits; Cayley unitary adapters executed on a 156-qubit IBM processor for Llama 3.1 8B) appeared in late 2025 and 2026, and all report parity-level results rather than advantage.

## Key question 5: Hardware demonstrations, their scale, and the honest comparisons to classical baselines

### Takeaway
Hardware generative-model demonstrations grew from 4-qubit BAS on trapped ions (2019) to 8 qubits (IonQ, 2021 to 2022), 16 qubits (IBM, KRAS drug discovery, 2024), 20 to 24 qubits (IonQ Forte, SAR change detection, 2026), 68 qubits (Google generative advantage, 2025), a 316-qubit IQP circuit decomposed onto 156-qubit IBM Heron chips (2026), 156-qubit IBM adapters for an 8B LLM (2026) and 2000 qubits on D-Wave Advantage2 (2026). Honest comparisons show: QCBMs win in data-scarce regimes (Gili 2023, Hibat-Allah 2024) but trainability degrades with size (Zhu 2022 follow-up, Rudolph 2024); annealer sampling is at best modestly different from MCMC for RBMs (El-Yazizi 2025, Dixit 2021); quantum LLM components reach parity, with 1.4 percent perplexity gains being the headline (Aizpurua 2026).

### Inferences
- Every claimed hardware "outperformance" is against a size-matched or convenience baseline (comparable GAN, equivalent classical learner, Gaussian-prior VAE, copula estimator); none is against the best classical generative model for the task, which is the standard Hibat-Allah et al. call "potential practical quantum advantage".
- The 2026 IonQ SAR paper is notable as the first application-driven QCBM result where hardware training plus inference beat the deployed classical baselines on a real dataset, but the F1 values (0.32 versus 0.24) are low in absolute terms.

### Gaps
- Exact qubit counts are absent from several abstracts (Coyle 2020, Kiss 2022, Duffield 2023, Sekwao 2026); the notes report only what the abstracts state.
- No hardware demonstration of a quantum transformer performing autoregressive text generation on a quantum processor was found; HyQuT does not state hardware execution in its abstract.
