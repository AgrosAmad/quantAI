# Quantum generative AI literature: surveys, theory, quantum priors and latents, tooling

Compiled 2026-11-03. Scope: 2017 to November 2026. Every identifier in the paper table was checked on a primary page (arXiv API or abstract page, Crossref, OpenAlex, OpenReview, publisher page or GitHub) during this compilation unless marked "unverified". Facts about the two reference papers below were read directly from the PDFs in `reference/`.

## How the two reference papers sit in the field

### Takeaway
Both papers belong to a small but growing "quantum prior / quantum latent" line in which a quantum device supplies the latent distribution of an otherwise classical deep generative model. Xiao et al. (2024) represent the trained-PQC-prior branch (the latent is learned jointly with a pretrained BigGAN), while Bacarreza et al. (2025/2026) represent the untrained-quantum-sampler branch with the most careful classical baselines to date (same photonic circuit with distinguishable photons) and the first complexity-theoretic argument for why such latents can help. Each cites the other's lineage: R1 cites Xiao et al. as ref. 19, and R2 cites ORCA's 2023 workshop precursor (Wallner and Clements, ICLR 2023 Physics4ML).

Source: [arXiv:2508.19857v3 PDF, Table IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19857v3)
- R1 synthetic quantum dataset: 8 photons in 16 modes with Haar-random unitaries, fully connected WGAN-GP-style generator and critic (2 hidden layers of 512), 12 runs, each drawing independent unitaries for the data, the quantum latent and the non-interfering-photon latent. Source: [arXiv:2508.19857v3 PDF, App. D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19857v3)

Source: [arXiv:2508.19857v3 PDF, Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19857v3)

Source: [arXiv:2508.19857v3 PDF, App. H](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19857v3)

### Inferences
- The two papers differ on the axis that R1 itself identifies as decisive: R2 trains the quantum prior jointly with the generator (so part of its gain may come from having a learnable latent at all), whereas R1 freezes the quantum sampler and controls for everything except quantum interference. Reading the two together, the open question is whether a trained quantum prior beats a trained classical prior of equal cost, which neither paper tests.
- R1's negative results and its own sensitivity analysis mean its headline QM9 numbers should be read as evidence that a benefit exists in some regimes, not as a general advantage; this matches the position papers on benchmarking (Schuld and Killoran 2022; Bowles, Ahmed and Schuld 2024) summarised in the surveys section.
- For local reproduction on two RTX 4090s, R2 is directly reproducible (TensorCircuit, 18-qubit blocks or MPS, two 24 GB GPUs were enough for the authors), while R1's size-48 boson-sampling latents required a 100-CPU cluster; the sizes 16 and 32 (8 photons in 16 modes; 16 photons in 32 modes) are the practical targets. Details in the tooling section.

## Part A: Surveys, reviews, books and position papers

### Takeaway
There is still only one comprehensive peer-reviewed survey of quantum generative models (Tian et al., IEEE TPAMI 2023), and no survey published through November 2026 has a category for "quantum device as the latent or prior of a classical generator"; the closest hooks are Tian et al.'s treatment of the quantum-Boltzmann-prior QVAE and Islam et al.'s (QIP 2026) "quantum-enhanced classical GAN" bin. The benchmarking position papers (Schuld and Killoran 2022; Bowles, Ahmed and Schuld 2024) set the standard against which the "quantum latent beats Gaussian latent" comparisons in both reference papers should be read.

## Part B: Theory of quantum generative modelling

### Takeaway
The theory supports R1's premise (boson-sampling latents are classically intractable in the ideal, large-n regime) and R1's theorem is a clean closure argument in the tradition of Coyle et al. 2020 and Du et al. 2020, but three strands bear down on both reference papers: (i) hardness-of-learning results (Hinsche 2021/2023; Nietner 2025) say typical Born distributions cannot be learned from samples by anyone, so a classical generator can only exploit a quantum latent when the latent already matches the data family; (ii) the 2025 to 2026 trainability and dequantisation results (Cerezo 2025; Herbst 2025; Herrero-Gonzalez 2025; Kurkin 2026) imply that a small trainable PQC prior such as R2's is almost certainly classically surrogatable, while an anticoncentrated sampler such as R1's could not have been trained at all, which vindicates R1's fixed-latent design; (iii) the lossy-photonics spoofing literature (Oh 2024; Villalonga 2021; Oszmaniec and Brod 2018; Garcia-Patron 2019) means R1's hardware-scale latents sit where classical imitations with matched low-order statistics exist, so R1's results cannot be attributed to intractability without a spoofer baseline.

### Cited Findings
Expressivity and intractability
- Coyle, Mills, Danos and Kashefi, "The Born supremacy: quantum advantage and training of an Ising Born machine" (npj QI 6, 60, 2020; arXiv 1904.02214; doi 10.1038/s41534-020-00288-9) shows IQP-type Born machine outputs cannot be classically sampled to multiplicative error unless the polynomial hierarchy collapses, and to additive error under average-case partition-function and anticoncentration conjectures, and trains the model with MMD, Stein and Sinkhorn losses on Rigetti hardware; the template for R1's hardness logic. Source: [arXiv 1904.02214](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02214)
- Du, Hsieh, Liu and Tao, "The expressive power of parameterized quantum circuits" (Phys. Rev. Research 2, 033125, 2020; erratum PRR 4, 029003, 2022; arXiv 1810.11922) shows simple PQCs generate distributions no polynomial classical network can sample unless PH collapses, with expressivity growing with entanglement and depth. No paper titled "Expressive power of parametrized Hamiltonians" was found; the brief's entry is most likely a conflation with this paper. Source: [arXiv 1810.11922](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11922)
- Gao, Anschuetz, Wang, Cirac and Lukin, "Enhancing generative models via quantum correlations" (PRX 12, 021037, 2022; arXiv 2101.08354) is the one separation not resting on complexity conjectures: a minimal quantum extension of Bayesian networks is strictly more expressive than classical Bayesian networks on the same graph, traced to nonlocality and contextuality. Sources: [arXiv 2101.08354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08354), [arXiv 2209.14353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14353), [arXiv 2402.08606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08606)
- Barthe, Grossi, Vallecorsa, Tura and Dunjko, "Parameterized quantum circuits as universal generative models for continuous multivariate distributions" (npj QI 11, 121, 2025; arXiv 2402.09848; doi 10.1038/s41534-025-01064-3; arXiv title "Expressivity of parameterized quantum circuits for generative modeling of continuous multivariate distributions") proves universality of expectation-value-sampling models (classical randomness uploaded to a PQC, fixed observables read out) with tight qubit and measurement bounds; this is the expressivity basis R2 cites for its continuous latent codes, and universality is shared with classical networks, so it supports feasibility rather than advantage. Learnability and hardness
- Sweke, Seifert, Hangleiter and Eisert, "On the quantum versus classical learnability of discrete distributions" (Quantum 5, 417, 2021; arXiv 2007.14451; doi 10.22331/q-2021-03-23-417): under the decisional Diffie-Hellman assumption an explicit class is not efficiently PAC-learnable (generative, TV distance) classically but is quantum-learnable via period finding; the mechanism is cryptographic structure that a generic variational circuit does not exploit. Source: [arXiv 2007.14451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14451)
- Hinsche et al., "Learnability of the output distributions of local quantum circuits" (arXiv 2110.05517, 2021; no journal record found): super-logarithmic-depth Clifford outputs are not sample-efficiently learnable in the statistical-query model by classical or quantum learners; with direct sample access Clifford outputs are classically PAC-learnable. Hinsche et al., "One T gate makes distribution learning hard" (PRL 130, 240602, 2023; arXiv 2207.03140, arXiv title "A single T-gate makes distribution learning hard"): one T gate in depth n^Omega(1) circuits makes density modelling hard under a standard cryptographic assumption, so simulability does not imply learnability, and generative modelling of such circuits is hard for any learner. Nietner et al., "On the average-case complexity of learning output distributions of quantum circuits" (Quantum 9, 1883, 2025; arXiv 2305.05765; doi 10.22331/q-2025-10-13-1883): random brickwork outputs are SQ-hard on average from depth omega(log n), need Omega(2^n) queries at some depth O(n), and are doubly exponentially hard at infinite depth. Since MMD, sample-estimated KL and discriminator losses are all SQ, these results cap any learning advantage for Born distributions. Sources: [arXiv 2110.05517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05517), [arXiv 2207.03140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03140), [arXiv 2305.05765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05765)
- The classical formalism R1 lifts: Hu et al., "Complexity matters: rethinking the latent space for generative modeling" (NeurIPS 2023 spotlight; arXiv 2307.08283) argues the optimal latent minimises a latent-to-data distance so generator capacity is best used; R1 replaces parametric capacity with sampling-complexity classes closed under Lipschitz generator maps and measured in W1. ### Inferences
- The consensus that formed in 2025 to 2026 is that advantage in generative modelling cannot come from training a generic PQC on a hard distribution; it can come from quantum data or quantum-origin tasks (Huang et al.), from a provable expressivity resource such as contextuality (Gao; Anschuetz), or from using the quantum device only as a sampler whose training signal is classically computable (Kasture; Recio-Armengol; Kurkin). R1 is a variant of the third route with no training on the quantum side at all; R2 belongs to the pre-2024 variational paradigm that the dequantisation results now bracket as surrogatable at small scale and untrainable at large scale.
- The missing experiment for R1, suggested by Gao et al. and Aaronson-Arkhipov 2014, is a classical latent with matched low-order statistics (or a Villalonga/Oh-style spoofer sample bank) as a baseline; R1's distinguishable-photon control removes interference but does not match the marginals.
- Kurkin et al.'s non-universality of basic boson-sampling Born machines is consistent with R1's design: the classical generator is a heavyweight, learned version of the post-processing Kurkin et al. need for universality.

### Gaps
- No dedicated theorem on the learnability of boson-sampling output distributions was found; the nearest results are Kurkin et al. 2026, the qubit-circuit hardness results and the lossy-simulation literature. This is a genuine open point for positioning R1.
- Peer-review status unknown as of 2026-11-03 (no Crossref record): Huang et al. 2509.09033, Herbst et al. 2512.24801, Herrero-Gonzalez et al. 2511.01845, Kurkin et al. 2603.11014, Recio-Armengol et al. 2503.02934, Hinsche et al. 2110.05517, LOWESA 2308.09109, Villalonga et al. 2109.11525.
- Theorem statements were taken from abstracts and prior knowledge, not full texts; in particular the exact cryptographic assumption in the Hinsche PRL and the theorem list in Gil-Fuster et al. ICLR 2025 should be checked against the PDFs before quoting.
- No advantage theorem for quantum diffusion models on classical data was found for 2025 to 2026.

## Part C: Quantum devices and quantum distributions as priors or latents of classical generative models

### Takeaway
The line has three generations: (i) 2017 to 2021, a quantum annealer or quantum Boltzmann machine supplies a trained prior over binary latents (Benedetti's quantum-assisted Helmholtz machine, Khoshaman's QVAE, Vinci et al., Wilson et al.); (ii) 2021 to 2025, a trained gate-model QCBM or PQC supplies the latent of a GAN or molecular generator (Rudolph 2022 on an ion trap, Li 2021 and Kao 2023 on QM9, Vakili 2025 with wet-lab validation, Xiao 2024 = R2 with a pretrained BigGAN); (iii) 2023 to 2026, an untrained quantum sampler supplies the latent and the comparison is controlled (Wallner and Clements 2023, Jin and Merz 2025 on IBM hardware, Bacarreza 2025/2026 = R1 on the ORCA PT-2, followed in 2025 to 2026 by Kailasanathan et al. and Engdal et al. on the same hardware). Quandela's photonic work makes the photonic circuit the generator rather than the prior, but its 2026 "train classically, deploy on a boson sampler" papers are the obvious route to trained photonic priors.

## Benchmarks and datasets

### Takeaway
The field's standard benchmarks are small: bars-and-stripes and cardinality-constrained bitstrings for Born machines, MNIST subsets and QM9 for hybrid GANs, synthetic log-normal, bimodal or Gaussian-mixture targets and a few financial series for qGANs, and HEP calorimeter or Monte Carlo data. R1's QM9 experiment has two direct quantum-GAN comparators (Li et al. 2021; Kao et al. 2023), and the only maintained open harnesses into which a quantum latent could be dropped are QUARK (BMW/Fraunhofer) and Xanadu's qml-benchmarks; the Zapata generalisation-metric chain (Gili 2024; Hibat-Allah 2024; Raj 2026) supplies the evaluation vocabulary (validity, novelty, coverage) that R1's "valid and unique" and "novel" counts implicitly follow.

## Part D: Software and GPU-simulation tooling

### Takeaway
R2 is reproducible with exactly its own stack (PyTorch BigGAN plus TensorCircuit, now TensorCircuit-NG, with 18-qubit state-vector blocks or an MPS circuit of bond dimension 16; the authors used two 24 GB RTX 3090s), and R1's latent sampler is a CPU problem (Clifford and Clifford sampling; the official repository depends only on numpy, numba and torch) so both RTX 4090s should go to the classical generator. For gate-model priors trained end to end, PennyLane lightning.gpu (adjoint, complex64) behind qml.qnn.TorchLayer reaches about 28 to 29 qubits per 24 GB card; forward-only state vectors reach 31 qubits in complex64 on one card and 32 across both (CUDA-Q mgpu or lightning.gpu MPI); 32 qubits in single precision does not fit in 24 GB (2^32 x 8 B = 32 GiB). Xanadu's photonic stack (Strawberry Fields, MrMustard, The Walrus) is archived as of 2026; Perceval, Piquasso and MerLin are the maintained photonic simulators, none with a verified GPU permanent backend except MerLin's README claim.

GPU memory and qubit limits for two RTX 4090s (24 GiB each)
- State-vector memory is 2^n x 8 B in complex64 and 2^n x 16 B in complex128: 28 qubits = 2 / 4 GiB, 29 = 4 / 8 GiB, 30 = 8 / 16 GiB, 31 = 16 / 32 GiB, 32 = 32 / 64 GiB. Forward-only on one card: 31 qubits in complex64 fits, 32 does not; 30 in complex128 fits, 31 does not. CUDA-Q's documented "< 33 qubits single precision, 32 double at 64 GB" matches this arithmetic. Source: [CUDA-Q state-vector sims](https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/latest/using/backends/sims/svsims.html)
- With gradients: adjoint differentiation needs the ket plus at least one bra-sized buffer, and lightning.gpu by default allocates one bra per observable (cap with batch_obs). Budgeting three state-sized buffers plus about 1 GiB of CUDA context, one 4090 trains about 29 qubits in complex64 or 28 in complex128; 30 qubits complex64 (3 x 8 GiB) will not fit. PyTorch-autograd simulators (TorchQuantum, TensorCircuit torch backend without checkpointing) store an intermediate state per gate, so memory scales as (depth + 1) x 2^n x 8 B; at 24 qubits complex64 (128 MiB per state) about 180 states fit in 23 GiB. The factor-three adjoint budget and the depth scaling are inferences from the docs, not documented numbers. Source: [Lightning GPU docs](https://docs.pennylane.ai/projects/lightning/en/stable/lightning_gpu/device.html)
- Pooling both cards (CUDA-Q mgpu or lightning.gpu mpi=True) distributes the state over 2^k GPUs, so two cards add exactly one qubit: 32 qubits complex64 forward-only, 31 in complex128, about 30 / 29 with adjoint. CUDA-Q starts distributing at 25 qubits by default and warns that without an NVLink-class fabric allocation may fail unless CUDAQ_GPU_FABRIC=NONE; consumer 4090s lack NVLink (background knowledge, not verified here), so global-qubit gates are PCIe-bound. Qiskit Aer's chunking rule gives blocking_qubits at most 28 (4 GiB chunks) in double precision on 24 GiB. Sources: [CUDA-Q state-vector sims](https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/latest/using/backends/sims/svsims.html), [Aer GPU how-to](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-aer/howtos/running_gpu.html)
- Tensor networks decouple qubit count from memory for shallow or low-entanglement circuits (CUDA-Q tensornet "thousands" of qubits, tensornet-mps "hundreds"); MPS memory is about n x chi² x 2 x 16 B, so R2's 120-qubit, chi = 16 circuit is about 1 MB and chi = 2048 about 15 GiB in complex128. Because lightning.tensor exposes only parameter-shift and finite differences, backprop-trained MPS priors are best run through TensorCircuit-NG or quimb with the torch backend, which is what R2 did. Sources: [CUDA-Q simulators](https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/latest/using/backends/simulators.html), [Lightning Tensor docs](https://docs.pennylane.ai/projects/lightning/en/stable/lightning_tensor/device.html)

Photonic and boson-sampling simulation
- Exact single-photon boson sampling: probabilities are |Perm(U_S)|² / prod(s_i!), permanents cost O(n 2^n) by Ryser or Glynn; Clifford and Clifford, "The classical complexity of boson sampling" (SODA 2018, pp. 146-155; arXiv 1706.01260; doi 10.1137/1.9781611975031.10) draws one exact sample in O(n 2^n + poly(m, n)) time and O(m) space, and "Faster classical boson sampling" (arXiv 2005.04214, 2020) lowers the average cost for m proportional to n. SLOS enumerates all C(n+m-1, n) output states: 490,314 for 8 photons in 16 modes (easy) but about 1.5 x 10^12 for 16 photons in 32 modes, so PT-2-scale distributions must be sampled, not enumerated. Per-sample cost of about n 2^n operations is roughly 2 x 10^3 (n = 8), 10^6 (n = 16), 4 x 10^8 (n = 24); R1's 3.75 hours on 100 EPYC 7003 processors for 10^6 size-48 samples is about 1.4 CPU-seconds per sample. A PT-2-sized problem is minutes of CPU per million samples, so no GPU is needed for the latent sampler. Sources: [arXiv 1706.01260](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01260), [arXiv 2005.04214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04214), [Perceval backends docs](https://perceval.quandela.net/docs/v1.3/backends.html)
- Lossy and approximate methods: Oh et al., "Classical simulation of lossy boson sampling using matrix product operators" (PRA 104, 022407, 2021; arXiv 2101.11234); Neville et al. (Nature Physics 13, 1153, 2017; arXiv 1705.00686; MCMC sampler); Villalonga et al. 2021 and Oh et al. 2024 (see Part B); Dodd et al., "A fast and frugal Gaussian boson sampling emulator" (arXiv 2511.14923, 2025); Gaussian boson sampling via hafnians in The Walrus and Piquasso, with Quesada et al. (PRX Quantum 3, 010306, 2022; arXiv 2010.15595) and Bulmer et al. (Science Advances 8, eabl9236, 2022; arXiv 2108.01622) mapping the boundary. Sources: [arXiv 2101.11234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11234), [arXiv 1705.00686](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.00686), [arXiv 2511.14923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14923), [arXiv 2010.15595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.15595), [arXiv 2108.01622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01622)
- GPU acceleration: no GPU backend was found in Perceval, Piquasso/PiquassoBoost or The Walrus; GPU permanents exist as research codes, e.g. Elbek and Kaya, "SUperman: efficient permanent computation on GPUs" (Comput. Phys. Commun. 321, 2026; arXiv 2502.16577; 62 x 62 permanent on 192 GPUs in 1.63 days). For differentiable photonic layers, Facelli et al. (arXiv 2409.16369) give exact gradients and MerLin provides PyTorch layers; "pyGBS" does not exist as a photonics package. Sources: [arXiv 2502.16577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16577), [arXiv 2409.16369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16369)

### Inferences
- Recommended local stack: (1) R2-style PQC prior plus pretrained GAN: PyTorch 2.x with TensorCircuit-NG (torch or jax backend) for the MPS PQC (120 qubits at chi 16 to 64 is trivial on one 4090) and vmapped 18-qubit state-vector blocks; for end-to-end adjoint training of a larger state-vector prior use PennyLane lightning.gpu (complex64, batch_obs) behind TorchLayer up to about 28 to 29 qubits per card; TorchQuantum for prototyping below about 20 qubits. (2) R1-style boson-sampler latent: run ORCA's quantum-latent-distributions code or Perceval's Clifford2017 backend on CPU, cache sample banks (R1 itself works from 1M cached samples), and spend both GPUs on the WGAN-GP, DDGAN or flow-matching models; MerLin plus Facelli et al. for trainable photonic layers. (3) Avoid new builds on Strawberry Fields, MrMustard, The Walrus (archived), Qadence (unmaintained), qujax (no release since 2023), the qulacs-gpu wheel (2022); treat TFQ as maintenance-only. (4) Use the two 4090s as independent workers for seeds and ablations; pooling them for one extra qubit is rarely worth the PCIe traffic.

### Gaps
- No canonical CUDA-Q paper; lightning.kokkos GPU details not read; cuStateVec per-gate workspace sizes not verified; the adjoint factor-of-three budget and autograd depth scaling are inferences.
- ORCA's Python SDK is not publicly downloadable and the cuTensorNet PT-2 simulator is unreleased; CUDA-Q's orca-photonics internals were not read; Piquasso's differentiable connectors and MerLin's GPU performance are unverified claims.
- No public code for Rudolph et al. 2022 or Chang et al. 2021/2023.
