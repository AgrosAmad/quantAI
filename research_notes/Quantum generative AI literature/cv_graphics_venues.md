# Quantum computing and quantum ML papers at computer-vision and computer-graphics venues, 2018 to November 2026

Scope: CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, WACV, BMVC, ACCV, 3DV, SIGGRAPH, SIGGRAPH Asia, Eurographics, SGP, TPAMI, IJCV, ACM TOG, TVCG, plus workshops. Date of check: 2026-11-03.

How the enumeration was done (so the reader can judge coverage):
- CVPR 2018 to 2026, ICCV 2019 to 2025 and WACV 2020 to 2026: the complete CVF open-access listings were downloaded and every paper title was matched against quantum, annealing, QUBO, adiabatic, qubit(s), D-Wave and Ising (word-boundary matches, "quantis/quantiz" excluded). Example listing pages: [CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/CVPR2022?day=all), [CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/CVPR2026?day=all), [ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/ICCV2025?day=all), [WACV 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/WACV2026?day=all), [CVPR 2020 (per-day pages)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/CVPR2020?day=2020-06-17).
- ECCV 2018 to 2024: the full ECVA list [ecva.net/papers.php](https://www.ecva.net/papers.php) was parsed the same way. ECCV 2026 papers were not on ECVA at the time of the check.
- CVF-hosted workshop proceedings: 810 workshop pages were crawled (CVPR 2018 to 2026, ICCV 2019 to 2025, WACV 2022 to 2026) via the menus such as [CVPR 2025 workshops](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/CVPR2025_workshops/menu) and [ICCV 2025 workshops](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/ICCV2025_workshops/menu).
- 3DV, ACCV, SIGGRAPH/SIGGRAPH Asia, TOG, TVCG, CGF, TPAMI, IJCV: Crossref title searches restricted by ISSN or container (for example the [TOG ISSN query](https://api.crossref.org/journals/0730-0301/works?query.title=quantum&filter=from-pub-date:2018-01-01)), the Eurographics Digital Library DSpace API ([query](https://diglib.eg.org/server/api/discover/search/objects?query=quantum)), Semantic Scholar venue filters (partially, rate-limited) and the arXiv API for identifiers and abstracts.
- BMVC: [BMVC 2023 proceedings](https://bmvc2023.org/proceedings/), [BMVC 2024 accepted papers](https://bmvc2024.org/programme/accepted_papers/), [BMVC 2025 accepted papers](https://bmvc2025.bmva.org/programme/accepted_papers/), the BMVC 2022 site and the BMVA archive pages for 2018, 2020, 2021.
- Every arXiv ID in this file was resolved through the arXiv API; every DOI through Crossref; every CVF/ECVA URL was fetched. Hardware statements come from abstracts plus a keyword scan (D-Wave model names, IBM, IonQ, QuEra, Qiskit, PennyLane, simulator) of the first 14 pages of each paper's PDF.
- dblp.org throttled all API requests (HTTP 429) during this session, so the dblp venue-filtered search suggested in the brief could not be used; the sources above replace it.

## Key question 1: Which quantum papers were accepted in the main tracks, venue by venue and year by year?

### Takeaway
Across CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, WACV, 3DV, BMVC and ACCV there are 30 main-track papers from 2020 to 2026 that actually use quantum computing (annealers, gate-model hardware or circuit simulators); none before 2020. Only one of them is a generative model (MosaiQ, a quantum GAN, ICCV 2023). On the graphics side there is exactly one SIGGRAPH-family technical paper (Quantum Ray Marching, SIGGRAPH Asia 2023 Conference Papers), one Technical Communication (SIGGRAPH Asia 2024), one TVCG paper (Towards Quantum Ray Tracing), one Computer Graphics Forum algorithm paper (Quantum Coin, 2020), no TOG paper and no SGP paper.

### Inferences
- The main-track footprint is small and concentrated: 30 quantum papers over seven years across all seven CV conferences, versus thousands of papers per year at CVPR alone. Combinatorial-optimization papers on D-Wave annealers dominate 2020 to 2023; from 2024 the mix shifts toward gate-model and hybrid QNN papers (ATP, QuCNet, QuEENet, ResQ, HQC-NBV, layered-QAS) that run on simulators or IBM/QuEra hardware.
- No quantum diffusion model, quantum VAE or quantum latent-generative paper has appeared in a CVPR/ICCV/ECCV/WACV main track through CVPR 2026 and ICCV 2025; the quantum generative work that reached TPAMI-level journals is either a survey (Tian et al.), a quantum-state generator (Chen et al.) or a medical detector built on a quantum diffusion backbone (STQD-Det).

### Inferences
- The year-to-year count is flat rather than growing; the composition is changing (from annealing-based optimization toward hybrid QNN classifiers and gate-model robust estimation), and from 2025 the new entrants come from outside the original MPI network.
- For a reader interested in quantum generative models, the CV/graphics venues are currently a near-empty source: MosaiQ, 3D-QAE and the TPAMI survey are the only must-reads there, and the generative action is in quantum-computing venues and arXiv.
