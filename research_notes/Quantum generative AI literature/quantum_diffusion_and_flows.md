# Quantum diffusion, score-based, flow-matching and normalizing-flow generative models (2022 to November 2026)

Scope and method. This note catalogues quantum and hybrid quantum-classical diffusion, score-based, flow-matching and normalizing-flow generative models published between January 2022 and 3 November 2026. Candidates were found by sweeping the arXiv API (title and abstract queries for "quantum diffusion", "denoising diffusion" + quantum circuit/computer, "flow matching" + quantum, "normalizing flow" + quantum circuit, "score matching"/"score-based" + quantum, "latent diffusion" + quantum, restricted to 2022-2026), by web search, and by following Crossref and Semantic Scholar records. Every arXiv identifier in this note was confirmed against arXiv API metadata (title, authors, dates); every DOI quoted was confirmed through the Crossref API or the arXiv journal-reference field. Where a venue comes only from Semantic Scholar it is marked as such. Where I could not confirm something it is marked "unverified". Primary texts (arXiv HTML) were read for the foundational papers and for the hardware demonstrations; abstracts were used for the rest.

Naming collisions to be aware of. "QDM" is used by at least three different papers (Parigi et al., Wang et al. few-shot, Venturelli et al.). "QFM / Quantum Flow Matching" names two unrelated methods: a quantum-circuit propagator for density matrices (Cui, Zhang, Tang) and a classical functional flow-matching model trained on spin Wigner functions (Hahm et al.). "QLDM / Quantum Latent Diffusion Model" is used both by De Falco et al. (images, VQC denoiser in a classical latent space) and by Li, Jing, Zhu, Wang (quantum states, classical diffusion in a quantum-autoencoder latent space). The candidate "Kwun, Park et al." in the brief is actually Kwun, Zhang and Zhuang (no author named Park); the candidate "Chen, Wang et al. hybrid quantum-classical latent diffusion" could not be matched to a single paper (see Gaps in KQ1).

## KQ1. Foundational quantum diffusion papers and what each proposed

### Takeaway
The field was founded by five 2023-2024 papers that split along one line: diffusion over classical data with a parametrised-quantum-circuit denoiser (Parigi et al.; Cacioppo et al.; Koelle et al.) versus diffusion over ensembles of quantum states (QuDDPM by Zhang, Xu, Chen, Zhuang; QGDM by Chen et al.), followed in 2024-2025 by the mixed-state variant (Kwun et al.), the latent-space image variant (De Falco et al.) and the first hardware-noise-as-resource demonstration (Parigi et al. 2025).

### Cited Findings
- Parigi, Martina and Caruso (arXiv 23 Aug 2023; Adv. Quantum Technol. 2024, 2300401, DOI 10.1002/qute.202300401) proposed three "quantum-noise-driven generative diffusion models" and argued that the noise of NISQ processors should be exploited as a resource to generate distributions "difficult or even impossible to express classically"; the paper includes a numerical example of a hybrid classical-quantum model — [arXiv:2308.12013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12013). The three models are cqgdm (classical Gaussian forward chain, PQC denoiser), qcgdm (depolarizing-channel forward process rho_{t+1} = (1-p) rho_t + p I/d, classical denoiser trained per step to maximise fidelity) and qqgdm (depolarizing forward, non-unitary backward in which a trainable PQC couples the qubit to an environment qubit that is traced out); the cqgdm demo uses a 4-qubit, 256-layer PQC on 2D points along the line y = x with T = 40 steps in PennyLane/PyTorch, evaluated by KL divergence with no final numerical value stated; the qcgdm/qqgdm tests are single-qubit with T = 5 — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12013v3).
- Zhang, Xu, Chen and Zhuang's QuDDPM (arXiv 9 Oct 2023; Phys. Rev. Lett. 132, 100602 (2024), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.132.100602; code at github.com/francis-hsu/quantgenmdl) scrambles an ensemble of quantum states toward Haar-random states with random unitary layers and denoises with parameterised circuits plus ancilla measurement, introducing "multiple intermediate training tasks as interpolation between the target distribution and noise to avoid barren plateau"; demonstrations cover a correlated quantum noise model, quantum many-body phases and the topological structure of quantum data — [arXiv:2310.05866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05866). Details from the paper: the forward scrambler alternates single-qubit rotations with all-to-all ZZ layers; T = 20 (1- and 2-qubit clustered states, 2-qubit correlated noise), T = 30 (4-qubit TFIM phases), T = 40 (1-qubit circular states); n_A = 1 or 2 ancillas; the loss is MMD with the fidelity kernel (Wasserstein with infidelity cost for the circular-state task); all data registers have 1 to 4 qubits (L = 4 to 12 layers); the error decomposition is E = E_diff + E_M + E_gen with E_diff ~ O(e^-T), E_M ~ O(1/(N sqrt m)) and the generalisation scaling O(1/(TN)) shown numerically with "the proof is an open problem"; all results are classical simulations (TensorCircuit, QuTiP, POT) — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05866v5).
- Cacioppo, Colantonio, Bordoni and Giagu's "Quantum Diffusion Models" (arXiv 26 Nov 2023) replace neural networks with PQCs "in order to directly generate quantum states", present full-quantum, latent-quantum and conditioned versions and run "a simplified version of the algorithm ... on real NISQ quantum hardware" — [arXiv:2311.15444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15444). Details: full model on MNIST 0/1 downsized to 16x16 and amplitude-encoded into 8 qubits with T = 15 and an infidelity loss; latent model on all ten digits with a pre-trained classical autoencoder, 8-dimensional latents in 3 qubits, T = 8; conditioned model adds 4 label qubits (7 total); reported FID/WaM of 256.6/240.3 (full), 41.4/25.8 (latent) and 38.2/13.5 (conditioned latent); no numerical classical baseline; parameter counts 2520, 510 and 1110 — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.15444). The same group's follow-up "Hybrid and hardware-oriented approaches for quantum diffusion models" appeared at IJCNN 2025 (DOI 10.1109/IJCNN64981.2025.11227283, pp. 1-8) — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/IJCNN64981.2025.11227283); its abstract states a DDPM-based hardware-oriented model demonstrated on real hardware and a DDIM-based hybrid model, and that "our results do not surpass classical diffusion models in the generation of classical samples" — [Sapienza IRIS record](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/1755867). No arXiv version exists (arXiv author search for Cacioppo returns only 2311.15444 among diffusion papers) — [arXiv author search](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Cacioppo%2C+Andrea&searchtype=author).
- Koelle, Stenzel, Stein, Zielinski, Ommer and Linnhoff-Popien's "Quantum Denoising Diffusion Models" (arXiv 13 Jan 2024) introduce two quantum diffusion models (the dense "Q-Dense" circuit and the quantum U-Net "QU-Net"), benchmark on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10, claim to "surpass the classical models with similar parameter counts in terms of ... FID, SSIM, and PSNR", and add a "consistency model unitary single sampling architecture" for one-step generation — [arXiv:2401.07049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07049). Published at the 2024 IEEE International Conference on Quantum Software (QSW), pp. 88-98, DOI 10.1109/QSW62656.2024.00023 — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/QSW62656.2024.00023). Details: amplitude embedding of images, angle-embedded labels on an ancilla; best guided MNIST 8x8 Q-Dense model has 47 layers on 7 qubits (about 987 parameters); unitary single sampling needs log2(64) = 6 qubits for 8x8 and 10 for 32x32; MNIST 8x8 guided models "achieving FID scores around 100, thus 20 points better than classical models" at about 1000 parameters; CIFAR-10 grayscale FID 395.47 (U-Net), 271.03 (QU-Net), 399.38 (Q-Dense); trained in PennyLane on a noise-free simulator — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07049v1).
- Chen, Zhao, Zhou, He, Sun and Situ's "Quantum Generative Diffusion Model" (QGDM; arXiv 13 Jan 2024; accepted in IEEE TPAMI, DOI 10.1109/TPAMI.2026.3718311) is "a fully quantum-mechanical model whose forward and backward processes are grounded in quantum channel theory": a non-unitary forward process takes any target state to the completely mixed state, a trainable backward process with partial trace and shared timestep-conditioned parameters recovers it, a resource-efficient version reduces auxiliary qubits, and simulations show QGDM "outperforms quantum generative adversarial networks on random pure- and mixed-state generation" with a task-specialised approach for Gibbs states — [arXiv:2401.07039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07039); the TPAMI record is vol. 48, pp. 14988-15002 (2026) — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/TPAMI.2026.3718311). Details: depolarizing forward rho_t = (1-alpha_t) I/d + alpha_t rho_{t-1} with a cosine schedule and T = 30; QGDM needs 2N qubits (N <= 4 tested), RQGDM runs up to N = 8; mixed-state fidelity at N = 4 is 0.992 vs 0.477 (QuGAN) and 0.676 (EQ-GAN); TFIM Gibbs states for N = 1-4, beta in {0.5,...,5}; a "low-loss shortcut" analysis shows that reading the output from the system register lets product unitaries copy the noisy input and fail at generation; TensorCircuit/TensorFlow simulations, no hardware — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07039v5).
- Kwun, Zhang and Zhuang's mixed-state QuDDPM (MSQuDDPM; arXiv 26 Nov 2024; Phys. Rev. A 111, 032610 (2025), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.111.032610) removes QuDDPM's "requirement of high-fidelity scrambling unitaries" by using depolarizing channels in the forward process and PQCs with projective measurements in the backward process, adding a cosine-exponent noise schedule, single-qubit random ancillas and superfidelity-based cost functions — [arXiv:2411.17608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17608); DOI confirmed — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1103/PhysRevA.111.032610). Details: tasks are 1-qubit clustered states (T = 6, L = 4), 1-qubit circular states (L = 8) and the 4-qubit TFIM phase (T = 6, L = 12, 2 ancillas); mean X-magnetisation of generated many-body samples is 0.940 with the cosine-square schedule vs 0.679 (cosine) and 0.427 (linear), target about 0.95; the paper claims "comparable performance" to QuDDPM without side-by-side numbers; simulations limited to four qubits — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17608v2).
- De Falco, Ceschini, Sebastianelli, Le Saux and Panella's "Quantum Latent Diffusion Models" (arXiv 19 Jan 2025; Quantum Machine Intelligence 6(2), art. 85, Dec 2024, DOI 10.1007/s42484-024-00224-6) compress images with a classical autoencoder and run the denoiser as variational circuits in latent space, reporting better metrics than a parameter-matched classical model and continued advantage "when considering small percentages of the dataset for training" — [arXiv:2501.11174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11174). Details: 10-dimensional latents, 10 qubits with angle encoding, three VQCs (latent, time embedding, their sum) plus a skip connection; parameter counts 330 (classical) vs 120-360 (quantum); MNIST FID 44.36 (classical) vs 40.40 (best quantum), Fashion-MNIST 90.37 vs 84.89, EuroSAT (2 classes, 28x28) 30.56 vs 20.11; on Fashion-MNIST quantum models trained on 20% of the data beat the classical model trained on 100%; noiseless simulation in PennyLane/Flax — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11174v1). The same group's earlier "Towards Efficient Quantum Hybrid Diffusion Models" (arXiv 25 Feb 2024; published as "Quantum Hybrid Diffusion Models for Image Synthesis", KI - Kuenstliche Intelligenz 38(4), 311-326, DOI 10.1007/s13218-024-00858-5) hybridises a classical U-Net with quantum ResNet blocks at the vertex and a quanvolutional filter in the encoder — [arXiv:2402.16147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16147); 12-qubit VQCs, MNIST FID 45.28 (classical) vs 43.07 (7HQConv), Fashion-MNIST 39.46 vs 38.8, with several hybrids not beating the classical model — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16147v1).
- Parigi, Martina, Venturelli and Caruso's 2025 follow-up (arXiv 28 May 2025; Quantum Mach. Intell. 8, 110 (2026), DOI 10.1007/s42484-026-00459-5) uses quantum stochastic walks in the forward process (lower MNIST FID than fully classical dynamics) and "an algorithm to generate images by exploiting the intrinsic noise of real IBM quantum hardware with only four qubits" — [arXiv:2505.22193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22193).
- Citation counts (Semantic Scholar, retrieved 3 Nov 2026): QuDDPM 78, Parigi 2023 55, Koelle 31, QGDM 25, De Falco QLDM 25, Cacioppo 22, Kwun 21, De Falco KI 20 — [Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2310.05866?fields=title,citationCount).

### Inferences
- The field's two lineages have different evaluation cultures: image-data papers report FID/SSIM/PSNR against small classical networks, while quantum-state papers report fidelity, MMD or Wasserstein distances against QuGAN-type baselines; cross-lineage comparisons are rare.
- QuDDPM is the reference point for almost all 2025-2026 quantum-state diffusion work (MSQuDDPM, chaotic QDM, conditioning, barren-plateau mitigation, information calibration all cite it as the baseline).

### Gaps
- The brief's candidate "Chen, Wang et al. hybrid quantum-classical latent diffusion" could not be matched: the closest papers are Yeter-Aydeniz et al. (MITRE, 2508.09903), Qian, Deng and Qian (2607.07072), Chen, Hou, Hu and Cai (2504.00034, a bottleneck VQC rather than latent diffusion) and Li, Jing, Zhu and Wang (QMI 2026, quantum-state latent diffusion). I found no paper with that author pair.
- I found no dedicated "quantum conditional diffusion" paper for classical data beyond the conditioned variants inside Cacioppo et al., Mauro et al. (QCU-Net) and Quinn et al. (quantum-state conditioning).

## KQ2. Main design axes

### Takeaway
Seven recurring design choices organise the literature: (a) classical diffusion with a PQC denoiser; (b) diffusion over quantum-state ensembles; (c) quantum noise channels or quantum walks as the forward process; (d) hybrid latent diffusion; (e) quantum-enhanced U-Nets; (f) diffusion or flows for quantum-state and thermal-state preparation; and (g) quantum algorithms that accelerate classical diffusion or flow sampling.

### Cited Findings
- (a) PQC denoiser on classical data: Parigi et al.'s cqgdm — [arXiv:2308.12013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12013); Koelle et al.'s Q-Dense — [arXiv:2401.07049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07049); Shah and Vatsa use pairwise Bell-state entangling to cut space complexity on MNIST and CIFAR-10 — [arXiv:2411.15973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15973); Zhang, Lu, Xiao and Shi replace the denoiser with "a trainable boson sampling circuit" and report "superior denoising efficiency compared to both selected quantum denoising diffusion models and classical diffusion models ... on the MNIST handwritten digit dataset under the same number of training parameters" (Adv. Quantum Technol. 9, e00750, DOI 10.1002/qute.202500750) — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/qute.202500750); Waller et al. replace feed-forward blocks of a denoising transformer with QNNs — [arXiv:2606.27561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27561); Zhang et al.'s quantum implicit neural network denoiser (Future Generation Computer Systems 173, 107875, DOI 10.1016/j.future.2025.107875) — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.future.2025.107875).
- (b) Diffusion over quantum states: QuDDPM scrambles with random unitaries and denoises with PQC plus ancilla measurement — [arXiv:2310.05866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05866); QGDM uses a depolarizing forward channel and a channel-based denoiser with partial trace — [arXiv:2401.07039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07039); Zhu et al.'s CCMQD models "diffusion and denoising steps as quantum channels defined by Kraus operators" optimised on the Stiefel manifold, reaching fidelities above 0.998 for up to 7-qubit entangled states — [arXiv:2511.12221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12221); Tran et al.'s chaotic quantum diffusion model generates "projected ensembles via chaotic Hamiltonian time evolution" needing "only global, time-independent control" for analog platforms — [arXiv:2602.22061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22061); Li et al.'s QGen interleaves Gaussian diffusion channels with unitary delocalisation and uses measurement-based training to mitigate barren plateaus — [arXiv:2506.10571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10571); Huang, Chen and Zhao's continuous-variable model uses a thermal loss channel forward and a CV quantum neural network backward, generating coherent, squeezed, Fock and cat states with fidelities above 99% — [arXiv:2506.19270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19270); Liu, Zhuang, Hou and You's measurement-based model uses randomised weak measurements and shows "quantum score matching is mathematically equivalent to learning unitary generators for the reverse process", with Petz recovery maps as quantum reverse Fokker-Planck equations — [arXiv:2508.08799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08799); Zhang et al. use a MERA denoiser for GHZ-like and W-like states (Phys. Scr. 100, 065120) — [arXiv:2505.10796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10796).
- (c) Quantum noise or quantum walks as forward process: Parigi 2023 (depolarizing channel) — [arXiv:2308.12013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12013); Parigi 2025 (quantum stochastic walk with a classical-quantum mixing parameter omega; omega = 0.3 gives lower mean FID than the classical walk omega = 1; the second protocol uses delay-induced hardware noise with a sine-squared schedule over T = 20) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22193v2); Venturelli et al. run a discrete-time quantum walk forward process on hardware with a classical backward U-Net — [arXiv:2609.31070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31070); Baidachna et al. replace Gaussian noise with random unitary matrices — [arXiv:2412.21082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21082); Chen, Hou, Hu and Cai introduce "intrinsic quantum-generated noise and a tailored noise scheduling mechanism" — [arXiv:2504.00034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00034).
- (d) Hybrid latent diffusion: Cacioppo et al.'s latent variant (classical autoencoder, 3 qubits) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.15444); De Falco et al. (10-qubit VQC in a 10-dim latent) — [arXiv:2501.11174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11174); Yeter-Aydeniz et al. (quantum residual blocks in both the VAE and the diffusion U-Net; 1024x1024 fundus images compressed to a 128x128 latent) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09903v1); Qian, Deng and Qian (classical autoencoder plus MSQuDDPM in latent space, with an analytic one-step reverse update predicting rho_0) — [arXiv:2607.07072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07072); Tran et al.'s fully quantum latent model denoises amplitude-encoded opcode latents with one mid-circuit measurement — [arXiv:2610.38974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38974); Guo et al. validate shot-efficient latent diffusion on MNIST — [arXiv:2610.41721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41721); Li, Jing, Zhu and Wang's QLDM combines vector quantisation and a quantum autoencoder with a classical diffusion model in the latent space to generate quantum states and "alleviate the barren plateau problem" (QMI 8, 10 (2026), DOI 10.1007/s42484-026-00352-1; code github.com/gxli2017/QLDM) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42484-026-00352-1).
- (e) Quantum-enhanced U-Nets: Koelle's QU-Net with one quantum convolution per block — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07049v1); De Falco's QVU-Net/QuanvU-Net — [arXiv:2402.16147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16147); Baidachna's VQC inside the U-Net — [arXiv:2412.21082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21082); Mauro et al.'s Quanvolutional Conditioned U-Net (QCU-Net) on EuroSAT, reducing FID by 64% and KID by 76% versus their classical counterpart (IEEE TGRS 64, 2026, DOI 10.1109/TGRS.2026.3661445) — [arXiv:2512.20448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20448); Jo et al.'s hybrid U-Net with adaptive non-local observables on full MNIST — [arXiv:2602.03405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03405); Wang et al. insert VQC layers in the bottleneck of a corrective diffusion U-Net for wind-field downscaling — [arXiv:2605.23403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23403); Kim and Yoo isolate the VQC in a squeeze-and-excitation scaffold — [arXiv:2607.09108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09108).
- (f) State and thermal-state preparation: QGDM's Gibbs-state task (TFIM, N = 1-4) — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07039v5); Cao et al.'s conditional QuDDPM "capable of generating ground states based on Hamiltonian parameters" — [arXiv:2512.06695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06695); Quinn et al.'s conditioning across "single-qubit generation tasks, entangled state preparation, and many-body ground state generation" with errors reduced by "more than an order of magnitude" — [arXiv:2509.17569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17569); Cui et al.'s QFM prepares states with prescribed magnetisation and entanglement entropy and estimates free-energy differences for the Jarzynski equality — [arXiv:2508.12413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12413); Zhu et al.'s structure-preserving (mirror) diffusion generates valid density matrices with classifier-free guidance — [arXiv:2404.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06336); Dong and Leng's quantum score matching learns Gibbs states "without additional thermal-state preparation" — [arXiv:2609.28391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28391).
- (g) Quantum algorithms for classical diffusion/flows: Wang, Jiang, Fan, Jia, Eisert, Liu and Liu implement DPM-solver-k and UniPC via quantum Carleman linearisation with quantum linear-system solvers or LCHS — [arXiv:2502.14252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14252); Layden et al. show flow models "are naturally related to the Schroedinger equation, for an unusual Hamiltonian" that can be simulated efficiently, yielding qsamples — [arXiv:2510.08462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08462); Che and af Klinteberg replace the QROM encoder of the wavefunction flow with a trained QNN in a phase-kickback circuit — [arXiv:2610.11537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11537); Chen et al.'s QD3PM learns the joint distribution of discrete diffusion in Hilbert space, proving classical per-dimension factorisation gives "worst-case linear scaling of KL divergence with data dimension" and enabling single-step sampling (npj Quantum Information 12, 49 (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41534-026-01188-0) — [arXiv:2505.05151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05151).
- Continuous-time theory of quantum reverse diffusion is a distinct 2025-2026 strand: Gabbassov derives "analytical stochastic Schroedinger equations for quantum reverse diffusion" for monitored Pauli channels (Phys. Rev. Research 8, 023329 (2026)) — [arXiv:2511.15919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15919); Bompais, Guta and Garrahan prove the denoising dynamics is a physical quantum diffusion with a "state-dependent feedback Hamiltonian, a direct analogue of the score function" — [arXiv:2606.03848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03848); Vu and Rebentrost derive the reverse SDE on complex projective space for any Lindbladian and bound generation error with exact and learned scores — [arXiv:2609.39861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39861); Dubey and John prove the Garcia-Pintos feedback Hamiltonian equals the score of the trajectory distribution — [arXiv:2604.21210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21210); Fayad shows that for Gaussian pure-loss dynamics "complete positivity forces irreducible reverse noise" above a critical squeezing-to-thermal ratio — [arXiv:2603.06488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06488); Nasu, Tanaka and Tsuchiya connect the Petz map to Bayes' rule and classical reverse diffusion (JPSJ 95, 104004) — [arXiv:2510.18512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18512); Qian and Qian propose information-calibrated schedules using the classical-quantum information decrement and report "stronger endpoint trace-Wasserstein performance than an official QuDDPM implementation with fewer trainable parameters" — [arXiv:2608.14083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14083), extended to channel-constrained scheduling — [arXiv:2609.26273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26273).

### Inferences
- Axis (b)/(c) papers increasingly treat the forward process as physics (channels, chaotic Hamiltonians, monitored dynamics) rather than as an engineered schedule, which makes them more natural for analog or noisy hardware but harder to compare with image-generation benchmarks.
- Axis (g) is the only strand with asymptotic complexity claims; everything else is empirical at 1-12 qubits.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that applies quantum diffusion to text, molecular graphs or general graph generation; searches for quantum diffusion with "text", "graph", "molecul*" and "language" returned only classical diffusion used to synthesise quantum circuits (Fuerrutter et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2024) — [arXiv:2311.02041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02041) — and a quantum-inspired reinterpretation of classical diffusion language models — [arXiv:2610.21672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21672).

## KQ3. Quantum normalizing flows and quantum flow matching

### Takeaway
Quantum normalizing flows exist in two forms (a unitary circuit used directly as the bijection, and PQCs embedded in classical coupling layers), both at proof-of-concept scale; quantum flow matching (Cui, Zhang, Tang; PRX Intelligence 2026) is the first circuit-level flow-matching analogue and already has conditional (CQFM) and single-step (Quantum MeanFlow, run on IBM hardware) extensions, while a parallel classical strand does flow matching or score matching intrinsically on the pure-state manifold.

### Cited Findings
- Rosenhahn and Hirche (arXiv 5 Feb 2024; Phys. Rev. A 110, 022443 (2024), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.110.022443) "introduce Normalizing Flows for Quantum architectures" and report competitive anomaly detection versus isolation forests, LOF and one-class SVMs — [arXiv:2402.02866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02866); DOI — [Crossref title query](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Quantum+Normalizing+Flows+for+Anomaly+Detection+Rosenhahn&rows=1). Details: the flow is the circuit unitary U = O(L)...O(1), bijective by unitarity, with no log-det Jacobian (the anomaly score is a KL divergence between the measured output and a target distribution); gates are chosen by Monte Carlo graph search over a fixed gate pool; datasets are Iris (4 qubits) and Wine (5 qubits) with 25-35 training samples; AUROC on Wine 2-3 is 0.95 (QF-KL) and 0.98 (QF-cos) vs 0.94 (SVM); no hardware, noise ignored by assumption — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02866v3).
- Zhang and Cui's hybrid quantum-classical normalizing flow (HQCNF; arXiv 22 May 2024) embeds a PQC in a coupling layer and derives the loss "in the case that quantum computing is involved", reporting lower FID than QGANs on image generation with fewer parameters than classical models — [arXiv:2405.13808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13808). Details: each layer splits inputs into three blocks (identity, classical affine, PQC U(phi) with angles from a classical network); unitarity gives a unit Jacobian block so the log-det comes only from the affine scale; MNIST digits 0 and 1 only; FID 1.77 (HQCNF) vs 4.80 (hybrid QGAN); no classical-flow FID; PyTorch/PennyLane simulation only — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13808v1). Martinez and Delgado apply "a previously developed hybrid quantum-classical normalizing flow" to lattice field theory sampling (IEEE QCE 2025, DOI 10.1109/QCE65121.2025.00266) — [arXiv:2505.10553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10553).
- Cui, Zhang and Tang's Quantum Flow Matching (arXiv 17 Aug 2025; PRX Intelligence 1, 013009 (2026), DOI 10.1103/s6zj-vzdp) is "a quantum-circuit realization that offers efficient interpolation between two density matrices", validated on magnetisation/entanglement-entropy targets, the quantum Jarzynski equality and superdiffusion — [arXiv:2508.12413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12413); venue — [Crossref title query](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Quantum+Flow+Matching+Cui+Zhang+Tang&rows=1). Details: no explicit velocity field; a sequence of step circuits V(theta_tau) with ancillas prepared as R_y(tau pi/T)|0> and measured to insert conditional single-qubit gates; entanglement-varied hardware-efficient ansatz; losses are task-specific (swap-test fidelity, MSE on entropy, energy); system sizes n = 1 (ring states), 2-3 (entanglement growth), 2-11 (magnetic transition), 6-qubit TFIM (Jarzynski), 10 qubits plus 2 ancillas (superdiffusion); QuDDPM is the main conceptual benchmark; simulation only, "experimental implementations of QFM" listed as future work — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12413v4).
- Joshi, Mistry and Koyama's Quantum MeanFlow (arXiv 2 Sep 2026) learns the average velocity over an interval for single-step generation, benchmarks QFM vs QMF on MNIST, and executes both "on IBM quantum computers" with best-of-N rejection sampling recovering most hardware loss — [arXiv:2609.02186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02186). Details: 11 qubits (5 data, 2 time, 4 class), MNIST 16x16 via a 32-dim classical autoencoder latent mapped to 5 angles; simulated accuracy 0.85 (QFM, 60 Heun steps) vs 0.63 (QMF, one step); ibm_boston single-sample accuracy 0.30 (QFM) vs 0.20 (QMF), rising to 0.80 and 0.60 with best-of-8; 4096 shots per basis; Keio University — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02186v1).
- Chen and Chen's Conditional Quantum Flow Matching (arXiv 12 Sep 2026) is "a single 306-parameter circuit, conditioned on both flow time and class label", claimed as the first conditional QFM and the first EEG augmentation on a PQC; on BCI Competition IV-2a starting from a class prior is worth +5.1 accuracy points over QuDDPM on 9/9 subjects, although "a class-conditional Gaussian matches CQFM" at that operating point — [arXiv:2609.14019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14019).
- Classical flows/scores on quantum-state manifolds: Hahm, Hur, Lee and Park's functional flow matching on spin Wigner functions (QTML 2024 extended abstract) — [arXiv:2607.00301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00301); Xu, Zeng, Paisley and Zhao's Intrinsic Flow Matching on CP^{d-1} with Pancharatnam phase-aligned paths, improving over ambient Euclidean flow matching "but not uniformly across every metric" — [arXiv:2606.21256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21256); Xu et al.'s local-time Riemannian score matching on CP^{d-1}, beating an ambient Euclidean baseline "by an order of magnitude" — [arXiv:2605.03573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03573).
- Quantum simulation of flows: Layden, Sweke, Havlicek, Chowdhury and Neklyudov (IBM/Mila) — [arXiv:2510.08462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08462); Che and af Klinteberg — [arXiv:2610.11537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11537).
- Peripheral flow papers that are often conflated with quantum flows: Q-Flow uses classical normalizing flows to model the Husimi Q function of open quantum systems (ICML 2023, PMLR 202:8879-8901) — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/dugan23a.html), [arXiv:2302.12235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12235); Lawrence, Shelby and Yamauchi build neural quantum states from normalizing flows — [arXiv:2406.02451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02451); Zou et al.'s Flow-VQE uses conditional normalizing flows to warm-start VQE (npj Quantum Information 12, 5 (2026)) — [arXiv:2507.01726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01726); Tuysuz, Kyriienko and Grossi's quantum Fourier generative model is not a flow but is compared against normalizing-flow and diffusion baselines, which "connect neighboring modes with spurious bridging density" or "broaden and merge the adjacent modes" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28483v1).

### Inferences
- Quantum normalizing flows have not yet produced a density-estimation result with a proper change-of-variables likelihood on a quantum circuit; both existing designs either drop the Jacobian (unitary flow) or confine it to the classical part (HQCNF). The "flow" in quantum flow matching is likewise a propagator between density matrices rather than a learned velocity field.
- The 2026 trend is to port classical sampling accelerators (MeanFlow, rejection sampling, shot-noise reuse) to quantum generative circuits to cut the sequential circuit-submission cost.

### Gaps
- No continuous-variable (photonic) quantum normalizing flow was found; the only CV generative-diffusion paper is Huang, Chen and Zhao (2506.19270), and the only photonic denoiser is the Gaussian-boson-sampling model (Wiley page blocked, details from the Crossref abstract only).
- No quantum flow-matching paper has yet been accepted at a NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR main track or workshop as far as I could find.

## KQ4. Hardware demonstrations

### Takeaway
Hardware runs are all inference-only (training is always classical simulation), use 4 to 12 qubits, and fall into three patterns: the quantum denoiser sampled on IBM devices (Cacioppo 2023, Koelle 2024, Joshi 2026), the forward noising process run on IBM devices with a classical denoiser (Parigi 2025, Venturelli 2026), and hybrid transformers sampled on IQM (Waller 2026); all other papers, including every PRL/PRA/PRX-Intelligence quantum-state diffusion paper, are simulation-only.

### Cited Findings
- Cacioppo et al. 2023: conditioned latent model for digits 0 and 1 on IBM ibm_hanoi, 4 qubits, 37 CNOTs, latent dimension reduced from 8 to 4, 3 denoising steps, 3.2 x 10^5 shots; training in noiseless simulation; noise shifted some amplitudes from about 0.1 to 0.2 and reduced sample variability — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.15444).
- Koelle et al. 2024: unguided unitary single-sample model for MNIST 8x8 digits 0/1 on "IBMQ's 7-qubit quantum hardware" (device unnamed), "roughly 40 seconds, excluding transpilation and queuing", 10000 shots, producing "distinguishable samples" while "older calibrated devices yielded less recognizable digits" — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07049v1).
- Parigi et al. 2025: forward quantum-walk process on ibm_brisbane with 4 qubits (3 position qubits for an 8-node cycle graph plus 1 coin qubit), 10^4 shots on hardware vs 10^5 on the simulator; backward denoiser is a classical MLP; 6903 MNIST digit-0 images at 28x28 with 8 gray levels; FID 169 for the IBM-noise model vs 114 for the simulated hybrid model — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22193v2); published in Quantum Mach. Intell. 8, 110 (2026) — [arXiv:2505.22193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22193).
- Venturelli et al. 2026: discrete-time quantum walk forward process on IBM ibm_torino (133 qubits), using 6 qubits (BloodMNIST 64x64 RGB, 3329 images), 7 qubits (BraTS2020 190x190 slices, 484 images) and 6 qubits (FractureMNIST3D 28x28x28 volumes, 1370 volumes), 8192 shots; classical U-Net backward; reported KL/FID/SSIM of 0.090/243.3/0.377 (BloodMNIST), 0.124/297.7/0.676 (BraTS2020), 0.011/89.70/0.564 (FractureMNIST3D) versus a reproduced discrete-state-space diffusion baseline; described as "a proof of concept" — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31070v1).
- Waller et al. 2026 (QDiffusion-TS): inference on the IQM Emerald processor with 128 shots per expectation value and 32-step sequences; Apple and Amazon daily stock series; each replaced feed-forward block drops from 33,088 to 36 parameters (total 589,030 to 461,254); Wasserstein distance about 44% lower than Diffusion-TS in simulation and about 89% lower on hardware for a 136-sequence sample; the authors note the model "does not account for" hardware noise during training — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27561v1).
- Joshi et al. 2026 (Quantum MeanFlow): QFM and QMF circuits with 10-11 qubits executed on IBM Heron r2 ibm_pittsburgh and Heron r3 ibm_boston, 4096 shots per basis, MNIST 16x16; hardware accuracies 0.30/0.20 rising to 0.80/0.60 with best-of-8; zero-noise extrapolation "gave no gain" — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02186v1).
- Dong and Leng 2026 (quantum score matching, a learning rather than generative task): 4-qubit TFIM plus 1 ancilla on ibm_pittsburgh, 256 randomised circuits x 32 shots per gradient step, relative Hamiltonian-parameter error reduced from 62-64% to about 10% at beta = 0.2 with no error mitigation — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28391v1).
- Tuysuz et al. 2026 (Fourier generative model compared against flows/diffusion): sampling on IBM ibm_aachen with 10^5 shots per target, about 300 microseconds per sample, authors state they "cannot sample accurately beyond roughly 30 qubits today" — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28483v1).
- Simulation-only with hardware noise models: Yeter-Aydeniz et al. (MITRE) use a PennyLane noise model matched to ibm_cleveland with 8- and 12-qubit circuits, 1000 shots and 2.5-10% readout error; "the next priority is testing the sampling performance on quantum hardware" — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09903v1); Wang et al. report "negligible impact from simulated device noise at the tested circuit scale, whereas real-hardware deployment remains limited by qubit availability and execution fidelity" — [arXiv:2605.23403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23403); QGDM uses two-qubit depolarizing noise at 0.5-1% in simulation — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07039v5).
- Explicitly simulation-only: QuDDPM (TensorCircuit/QuTiP/POT, max 4 data qubits) — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05866v5); MSQuDDPM ("simulations reach only four qubits because of classical resource limits") — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17608v2); QFM — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12413v4); De Falco QLDM ("assumes a noiseless quantum computing environment") — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11174v1); Rosenhahn QNF — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02866v3); HQCNF — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13808v1).

### Inferences
- Every hardware result so far is a feasibility check rather than a benchmark: sample quality on hardware is consistently worse than in simulation (FID 169 vs 114; accuracy 0.30 vs 0.85), and the gap is closed only by classical post-processing (best-of-N) or by keeping the learned part classical.
- Datasets on hardware are MNIST-class only (8x8 to 28x28 digits, two-class subsets or digit 0), small medical image sets, or short financial series; no CIFAR, molecular or quantum-state-ensemble result has been run on hardware.

### Gaps
- Koelle et al. do not name the IBM device; Waller et al. do not state the Emerald qubit count or the hardware circuit width; Tuysuz et al. do not state the qubit count used (about 12 is my inference from 8 visible plus 4 hidden qubits).
- Cacioppo et al.'s IJCNN 2025 paper reports a further hardware run but I could not access the full text (IEEE paywall; the IRIS record returned HTTP 403 to my fetch).

## KQ5. Comparisons to classical baselines and limitations flagged

### Takeaway
Claimed advantages are confined to parameter-matched comparisons against very small classical networks (FID gains of 5-20 points on MNIST-scale data); the two most careful 2026 studies find no parameter-efficiency advantage once classical controls are parameter-matched (Kim and Yoo) and a clear loss to a classical latent diffusion model with prohibitive post-selection cost (Tran et al.), while authors across the field flag measurement/tomography overhead, sequential circuit submission, shot noise, simulation-bounded scale (4-12 qubits) and barren plateaus.

### Cited Findings
- Positive parameter-matched claims: Koelle et al. report MNIST 8x8 FID "around 100, thus 20 points better than classical models" at about 1000 parameters, but also "slow execution" (QU-Net 134 s per epoch and 859 s per sampling run vs 2 and 9 s for Q-Dense), mode collapse on CIFAR-10, and that "classical models still hold an edge on inpainting" — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07049v1). De Falco QLDM beats a 330-parameter classical model on FID with 120-360 quantum parameters but the classical model keeps the best KID and IS on Fashion-MNIST at the end of training — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11174v1). De Falco KI: 7HQConv FID 43.07 vs 45.28 classical, with other hybrids slightly worse and hybrid training "significantly time consuming" — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16147v1). Yeter-Aydeniz: 85.9% gradable images vs 68.9% classical, but FID favours the larger classical model (29.51 vs 29.61) and CMMD favours the classical model (0.091 vs 0.218); the quantum layers add 216 parameters versus 16,384 for the size-matched classical block — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09903v1). Mauro et al.: FID reduced by 64% and KID by 76% on EuroSAT — [arXiv:2512.20448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20448). Waller et al.: about 44% lower Wasserstein distance with about 20% fewer total parameters, but "the classical model is marginally closer on skewness and kurtosis" and no clear low-data advantage — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27561v1). Baidachna et al.: quantum and hybrid models "competitive with a similar classical model" on LHC jets — [arXiv:2412.21082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21082). QD3PM: "superior accuracy in modeling complex distributions compared to factorization" baselines — [arXiv:2505.05151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05151). QGen: "outperforms classical and hybrid baselines under matched parameter budget" — [arXiv:2506.10571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10571).
- Negative or null results: Kim and Yoo find quantum cores "achieve comparable mean FID to the classical control across DDPM and latent diffusion", that "parameter-matched classical controls attain comparable mean FID, so the experiments do not establish a quantum parameter-efficiency advantage", and that score-based NCSN targets proportional to 1/sigma drive angle embeddings "far beyond the 2 pi period of rotation gates, causing phase aliasing and collapse" (fixed by theta <- pi tanh(.)) — [arXiv:2607.09108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09108). Tran et al.: the fully quantum latent model lowers denoising infidelity by 63% yet classifiers trained on its malware records reach macro-F1 0.11 vs 0.44 for a comparably sized classical latent diffusion model, post-selected sampling "accepts 3.0 x 10^-7 of runs", and a penalty raising acceptance to 0.14 "removes most of the denoising" — [arXiv:2610.38974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38974). Cacioppo et al. (IJCNN 2025): "our results do not surpass classical diffusion models in the generation of classical samples" — [IRIS record](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/1755867). Cacioppo et al. 2023 report no numerical classical baseline — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.15444). Chen and Chen: "a class-conditional Gaussian matches CQFM" at the main operating point — [arXiv:2609.14019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14019).
- Barren plateaus: Cao, Zhang, Tao and Su "demonstrate that this framework [QuDDPM] is currently restricted to small-scale systems. As the system size increases, a severe barren plateau problem emerges", prove its origin is "distinct from previously known causes" and propose an architecture restoring trainability (Phys. Rev. A 114, 022418 (2026), DOI 10.1103/gg2j-xtk6) — [arXiv:2512.06695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06695). QuDDPM's own claim is that splitting Omega(n) layers into T in Omega(n/log n) steps gives order log(n) layers per step, cited from prior work and not derived — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05866v5). Parigi 2023 note that a KL loss "may create a new flavour of barren plateaus" — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12013v3). Cacioppo 2023 report no barren plateaus despite 100-layer circuits — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.15444). QGen and Li et al.'s QLDM cite barren-plateau mitigation as a design goal — [arXiv:2506.10571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10571), [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42484-026-00352-1).
- Measurement, tomography and shot overhead: QuDDPM needs fidelity estimation that "can be challenging to implement" at scale — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05866v5); MSQuDDPM adopts superfidelity because full mixed-state fidelity "may require tomography with many state copies" — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17608v2); Cacioppo's branch selection has success rate 0.5 per measurement and six measurements yield 64 possible final states needing about 500 shots each — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.15444); Koelle note training on hardware is infeasible "due to state tomography costs" and outputs are always normalised, limiting fidelity — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07049v1); Guo et al. turn shot noise into sampler noise and reach comparable quality with 303 shots per sample against a 1.6 x 10^6-shot reference — [arXiv:2610.41721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41721); Joshi et al. motivate single-step QMF by "high input/output costs" of sequential circuit submission — [arXiv:2609.02186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02186).
- Scaling limits: simulation caps at 4 data qubits (QuDDPM, MSQuDDPM), 2N qubits for QGDM (N <= 4) — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07039v5); De Falco QLDM chose 10 qubits because 12 "significantly increases the required computational cost" — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11174v1); QFM's generalisation bounds "contain unspecified O(.) terms" — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12413v4); Fayad proves exact Gaussian reversal of a pure nonclassical endpoint "is dynamically unattainable" — [arXiv:2603.06488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06488).
- Theoretical cost of quantum DPM solvers: Wang, Eisert, Liu et al. present quantum ODE solvers as "one of the most direct and pragmatic applications of quantum algorithms to large-scale machine learning models" without hardware claims — [arXiv:2502.14252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14252).

### Inferences
- The strongest published evidence of benefit is in the few-shot regime (De Falco QLDM at 20-40% of data; Yeter-Aydeniz quarter-dataset; Chen et al. under 100 images), which is consistent with the quantum models simply being small, strongly regularised function classes rather than with a quantum-specific mechanism.
- The field lacks a shared benchmark; FID on 8x8 or 16x16 MNIST with Inception features is acknowledged by several authors as a weak metric (Venturelli et al. say FID "is a weak metric here").

### Gaps
- No paper reports wall-clock or energy comparisons for sampling on hardware versus a classical GPU; Waller et al. explicitly do not report latency.

## KQ7. The 2025 and 2026 papers

### Takeaway
2025-2026 output splits into (i) hardening the quantum-state diffusion lineage (conditioning, barren-plateau fixes, channel-constrained and chaotic forward processes, CV and measurement-based variants, information-calibrated schedules), (ii) continuous-time reverse-diffusion theory, (iii) hybrid applied models with hardware runs (medical imaging, finance, Earth observation, EEG), (iv) quantum flow matching and its variants, and (v) critical fair-comparison and negative-result studies.
