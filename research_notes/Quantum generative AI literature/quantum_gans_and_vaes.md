## KQ1. Foundational QGAN papers and what each proposed

### Takeaway
The field starts with two companion April 2018 preprints (Lloyd and Weedbrook: the theory and fixed-point argument; Dallaire-Demers and Killoran: circuits and quantum gradient estimation), then splits within a year into (i) quantum-data QGANs for state learning and (ii) hybrid QGANs with a quantum generator and classical discriminator for classical distributions (Zeng, Situ, Romero, Zoufal), with the first superconducting hardware proof of principle (Hu et al.) and the first image-generation experiment (Huang et al. 2021) following shortly after.

### Cited Findings
- Lloyd and Weedbrook introduced "quantum generative adversarial networks (QuGANs)", where the data are either quantum states or classical data and both generator and discriminator have quantum processors; they proved the unique fixed point of the quantum adversarial game is where the generator reproduces the data statistics, and argued for a possible exponential advantage when data are measurement samples on high-dimensional spaces. Phys. Rev. Lett. 121, 040502 (2018). — [arXiv:1804.09139](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09139)
- Dallaire-Demers and Killoran (Xanadu), in the stated companion paper, showed how to build GANs from quantum circuits, how to compute the gradients needed for adversarial training with another quantum circuit, gave a simple practical ansatz and a numerical demonstration that QGANs can be trained. Phys. Rev. A 98, 012324 (2018). — [arXiv:1804.08641](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08641)
- Benedetti et al. derived an adversarial algorithm for approximating an unknown pure quantum state with two parametrized circuits trained in tandem (resilient backpropagation; entanglement-entropy stopping heuristic), aimed at near-term devices and state tomography. New J. Phys. 21, 043023 (2019). — [arXiv:1806.00463](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00463)
- Zeng, Wu, Liu, Wang and Hu coupled a quantum-circuit generator to a classical neural-network discriminator ("generative adversarial quantum circuits"), showed the trained circuit can infer missing data with a quadratic speed-up via amplitude amplification, and tested on Bars-and-Stripes. Phys. Rev. A 99, 052306 (2019). — [arXiv:1808.03425](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03425)
- Situ et al. proposed a hybrid QGAN for classical discrete distributions (PQC generator with one-qubit rotations and controlled-phase gates; classical discriminator), motivated by text-like discrete data and by avoiding the input/output encoding bottleneck. Information Sciences 538, 193-208 (2020); first posted July 2018. — [arXiv:1807.01235](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01235)
- Hu et al. reported "the first proof-of-principle experimental demonstration" of quantum adversarial learning in a superconducting circuit: a quantum state generator trained to replicate the statistics of a digital qubit-channel simulator, reaching 98.8% average fidelity. Sci. Adv. 5, eaav2761 (2019). — [arXiv:1808.02893](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02893)
- Romero and Aspuru-Guzik proposed "variational quantum generators" for continuous distributions: a quantum encoder loads a classical random variable, a variational circuit is trained adversarially, and samples are read out as expectation values optionally post-processed by a classical network. Adv. Quantum Technol. (2020/2021), DOI 10.1002/qute.202000003 (Crossref-verified). — [arXiv:1901.00848](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.00848)
- Zoufal, Lucchi and Woerner (IBM) framed the qGAN as an approximate state-loading method: a variational circuit plus classical discriminator learns a distribution from samples and loads it into an n-qubit state with O(poly(n)) gates, enabling downstream quantum amplitude estimation; implemented in Qiskit and run on IBM Q Experience processors, with a quantum-finance demonstration in simulation. npj Quantum Inf. 5, 103 (2019). — [arXiv:1904.00043](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00043)
- Huang, Wang, Song et al. realised a QGAN on a programmable superconducting processor in which both generator and discriminator are parameterised quantum circuits trained with quantum gradients to a Nash equilibrium. npj Quantum Inf. 7, 165 (2021). — [arXiv:2009.12827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12827)
- Huang, H.-L. et al. (21 authors) devised a flexible QGAN scheme that can handle arbitrarily high-dimensional features and exploit superposition to train several examples in parallel, and "for the first time" experimentally learned and generated real-world handwritten-digit images on a superconducting processor; on a grey-scale-bar dataset they benchmarked against MLP and CNN GANs with the Fréchet distance. Phys. Rev. Applied 16, 024051 (2021). This is the paper usually called the "patch QGAN" (memory, not re-verified: the sub-generator-per-patch and superposition-batch strategies are named "patch" and "batch" in the paper). — [arXiv:2010.06201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06201)
- Chakrabarti et al. proposed the first quantum Wasserstein GAN with a Wasserstein semimetric between quantum data, intended to improve robustness and scalability of adversarial training of quantum generative models on noisy hardware (memory, not re-verified: presented at NeurIPS 2019; no DOI in arXiv metadata). — [arXiv:1911.00111](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00111)
- Du, Hsieh and Tao gave the first efficient online quantum generative adversarial learning algorithm (quantum multiplicative matrix weights), with complexity polynomial in training rounds and logarithmic in input size. Phys. Rev. Lett. 128, 110501 (2022); posted 2019. — [arXiv:1904.09602](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09602)

### Inferences
- The two 2018 companion papers define the two still-dominant readings of "QGAN": a quantum-data game whose target is a state (Benedetti, Hu, Huang K., EQ-GAN lineage) and a sample-based hybrid game whose target is a classical distribution (Zeng, Situ, Romero, Zoufal lineage). Most later application work is in the second lineage.
- Zoufal et al. is the de facto reference implementation (Qiskit) and the origin of the "distribution loading" framing that later finance and HEP papers (Agliardi and Prati, Certo, Chang) build on.

### Gaps
- Qubit counts for the Zoufal, Hu and Huang experiments are not stated in the abstracts and were not re-verified from full texts.

## KQ2. Main architectural variants

### Takeaway
Variants fall into: hybrid quantum generator plus classical discriminator (the workhorse), fully quantum GANs (mostly for quantum data), patch-based image QGANs (Huang 2021, PQWGAN, IQGAN, MosaiQ), style-based QGANs (Bravo-Prieto, Baglio), latent-space QGANs that compress with a classical autoencoder (LaSt-QGAN, LatentQGAN, VAE-QWGAN, LaSt-QWGAN), conditional QGANs (Certo, Pham), Wasserstein and quantum-Wasserstein QGANs (Chakrabarti, Kiani, Herr, Tsang), photonic and trapped-ion implementations, and a thin 2024 to 2026 line on classical-shadow or classically surrogated generators; I found no paper whose QGAN generator is a tensor network.

### Cited Findings
Hybrid quantum generator with classical discriminator
- Hybrid architecture originates with Zeng et al. (quantum-circuit generator, classical NN discriminator) — [arXiv:1808.03425](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03425) — and Situ et al. — [arXiv:1807.01235](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01235); Zoufal et al. made it the standard distribution-loading tool — [arXiv:1904.00043](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00043).
- Anand, Romero, Degroote and Aspuru-Guzik studied noise robustness of hybrid QGANs (HQGANs) for continuous distributions, found performance "unaffected" by injected generator noise in simulation, and trained on Rigetti's Aspen-4-2Q-A QPU. Adv. Quantum Technol., DOI 10.1002/qute.202000069. — [arXiv:2006.01976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01976)
- Li, Topaloglu and Ghosh (QGAN-HG) estimated that a fully quantum GAN would need more than 90 qubits even for QM9-like small molecules, and instead used a qubit-efficient hybrid generator that learned the molecular distribution with only 14.93% of the classical parameters; a patched-circuit variant accelerated training and avoided vanishing gradients; code on GitHub. IEEE Trans. Quantum Eng. (2021), DOI 10.1109/TQE.2021.3104804 (Crossref-verified). — [arXiv:2101.03438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03438)
- Kao et al. substituted VQCs into the noise generator and the discriminator of a molecular GAN; a VQC with "tens of learnable parameters" in the generator produced molecules with better physicochemical properties and goal-directed benchmark scores than the classical counterpart, and a tens-of-parameter quantum discriminator beat the MLP discriminator on KL divergence. J. Chem. Inf. Model. (2023), DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.3c00562. — [arXiv:2210.16823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16823)
- Herr, Obert and Rosenkranz replaced the generator of a Wasserstein GAN with a hybrid quantum-classical network, kept the classical critic so high-dimensional data never enters the circuit, and matched classical F1 on a credit-card fraud anomaly-detection task. Quantum Sci. Technol. 6, 045004 (2021). — [arXiv:2010.10492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10492)
- Nakaji and Yamamoto proposed a quantum semi-supervised GAN for classification. Sci. Rep. 11, 19649 (2021). — [arXiv:2010.13727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13727)
- Agliardi and Prati tuned qGANs for multivariate distribution loading. Quantum Reports (2022), DOI 10.3390/quantum4010006 (Crossref-verified; no arXiv version found). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/quantum4010006)
- Riofrío et al. systematically compared continuous versus discrete PQC architectures trained as QCBMs or QGANs against classical networks on six synthetic and two financial datasets, finding quantum models needed similar or fewer parameters (up to two orders of magnitude fewer). ACM Trans. Quantum Comput. 5(2) (2024). — [arXiv:2301.09363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09363)

Fully quantum GANs (quantum generator and quantum discriminator)
- Benedetti et al. (pure-state approximation) — [arXiv:1806.00463](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00463); Hu et al. (superconducting experiment) — [arXiv:1808.02893](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02893); Huang K. et al. (both networks as PQCs on a superconducting processor) — [arXiv:2009.12827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12827).
- Stein et al. (QuGAN) ran both discriminator and generator "purely on quantum state fidelity" using swap tests, reporting a 94.98% parameter reduction versus classical GANs at similar performance and a 48.33% improvement over prior quantum GANs; code released. IEEE QCE 2021, DOI 10.1109/QCE52317.2021.00023. — [arXiv:2010.09036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09036)
- Niu et al. (Google) introduced the entangling QGAN (EQ-GAN), which performs entangling operations between generator output and true quantum data, guarantees convergence to a Nash equilibrium under minimax optimisation, is robust to coherent errors, was demonstrated on the Sycamore processor, and was used to prepare an approximate QRAM. Phys. Rev. Lett. 128, 220505 (2022) (DOI Crossref-verified). — [arXiv:2105.00080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00080)
- Rasmussen and Zinner extended EQ-GAN to multi-qubit state learning (VQE states of small molecules; random states up to six qubits). Phys. Rev. A 106, 032429 (2022). — [arXiv:2204.09689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09689)
- Chaudhary et al. built a fully quantum GAN for binary data with noise re-uploading in the generator, auxiliary qubits in the discriminator and a direct generator-to-discriminator connection that avoids reading out the generator's distribution; tested on synthetic data and Ising low-energy states. — [arXiv:2209.13993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13993)
- Rehm et al. (CERN) demonstrated a small-prototype "full quantum GAN" for downsized calorimeter simulations. — [arXiv:2305.07284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07284)
- Kim, Lloyd and Marvian proposed Hamiltonian QGANs that use two competing quantum optimal controls instead of circuits. — [arXiv:2211.02584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02584)
- Bartkiewicz et al. proposed a cooperative ("synergic") alternative to adversarial quantum generative learning and ran a Bell-state learning experiment on a programmable quantum computer. Sci. Rep. 13, 12893 (2023). — [arXiv:2112.13255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13255)
- Frkatovic et al. (2025) analysed fully quantum generator plus discriminator image QGANs and found they converge to the average of the training data; for pure-state generators they derived a lower bound on discriminator quality given by the fidelity between generator output and target distribution. — [arXiv:2508.09844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09844)

Patch-based image QGANs
- Huang H.-L. et al. 2021 is the origin of patch-style image generation on hardware — [arXiv:2010.06201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06201).
- Tsang, West, Erfani and Usman (the PQWGAN; name from memory, not re-verified) generated 28 x 28 grey-scale MNIST and Fashion-MNIST images "without dimensionality reduction or classical pre/post-processing", comparable to classical frameworks with three orders of magnitude fewer trainable generator parameters, and swept qubits, patch size and shape, depth and prior. IEEE Trans. Quantum Eng. (2023), DOI 10.1109/TQE.2023.3319319. — [arXiv:2212.11614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11614)
- Chu et al. (IQGAN) attributed poor QGAN image quality to the input encoder and proposed a trainable multi-qubit encoder plus compact generator, validated on IBM processors and simulators. — [arXiv:2210.16857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16857)
- Silver et al. (MosaiQ) proposed a high-quality quantum image GAN framework executable on NISQ computers. ICCV 2023, DOI 10.1109/ICCV51070.2023.00647 (Crossref title match). — [arXiv:2308.11096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11096)
- Silver et al. (OrganiQ) claim the first QGAN producing high-quality images without any classical neural network. — [arXiv:2409.19823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19823)
- Chu, Skipper and Swany (LSTM-QGAN) argued PCA preprocessing hurts QGANs and patch-splitting does not scale, and used quantum LSTM to cut qubits 5x, single-qubit gates 5x and two-qubit gates 12x while improving FID. — [arXiv:2409.02212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02212)
- Khatun et al. (QIGL) replaced patches with principal components plus a Wasserstein loss and reported the lowest FID among classical and QGAN baselines on knee-osteoarthritis X-rays and medical MNIST. Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol. 6, 025032 (2025). — [arXiv:2406.13196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13196)
- Zhou et al. proposed hybrid QGANs for images via discrete-distribution learning. Signal Processing: Image Communication (2023), DOI 10.1016/j.image.2022.116891 (Crossref-verified; paywalled; no arXiv version found). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.image.2022.116891)
- The 2026 Xue Yang et al. series abandons patches for single-circuit end-to-end generation: ReQGAN synthesises an N = 2^D pixel image with one D-qubit circuit via a learnable Neural Noise Encoder and Intensity Calibration — [arXiv:2603.18554](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18554); CoQui reads each pixel as the expectation of a dedicated colour qubit queried at spatial coordinates, decoupling resolution from address qubits and beating FRQI-based generation and PQWGAN — [arXiv:2608.11884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11884); BasicQGAN calibrates the prior-induced "quantum fidelity landscape" before training — [arXiv:2609.36702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36702).

Style-based QGANs
- Bravo-Prieto et al. (style-qGAN) proposed a quantum generator architecture for LHC Monte Carlo event generation that reached smaller KL divergences with shallow circuits and small training sets, and deployed it on trapped-ion and superconducting hardware. Quantum 6, 777 (2022). — [arXiv:2110.06933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06933)
- Baglio benchmarked a fixed style-based qGAN for HEP data augmentation on IBM ibm_torino (Heron) and IonQ aria-1. AIP Advances 16, 065008 (2026). — [arXiv:2405.04401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04401)
- Style-based noise re-uploading at every rotation gate is also the generator design in the 2026 LaSt-QWGAN for drug design. — [arXiv:2603.22399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22399)

Latent-space QGANs
- Chang et al. (LaSt-QGAN, CERN) map images into a classical autoencoder latent space, run a style-based hybrid QGAN there, and decode; trained on Fashion-MNIST and SAT4 with 10 qubits at performance comparable to (sometimes better than) classical GANs, with a barren-plateau analysis and mitigation for deep circuits. — [arXiv:2406.02668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02668)
- Vieloszynski et al. (LatentQGAN) couple a hybrid QGAN to a classical convolutional autoencoder and report gains over existing quantum methods on simulators and NISQ computers with reduced quantum resources. — [arXiv:2409.14622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14622)
- Thomas et al. (VAE-QWGAN; the API metadata lists "Aaron Mark Thomas" plus two co-authors whose names I did not capture) fuse a classical VAE decoder with a hybrid quantum Wasserstein GAN generator, sample a learned Gaussian-mixture prior at inference, and report significantly reduced mode collapse on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST. — [arXiv:2409.10339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10339)
- Baglio, Khan and Vallecorsa (LaSt-QWGAN) combine a molecular VAE latent space with a hybrid quantum Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty; circuits up to 15 qubits in simulation, an inference-only run on an IBM Heron device with 5 qubits sampling 2,500 molecules, and a 110-parameter quantum generator statistically comparable to the ~700,000-parameter MOSES latent-GAN baseline. J. Chem. Inf. Model. 66(20), 13136 (2026). — [arXiv:2603.22399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22399)
- Liepelt and Baglio report that in the hybrid latent style-based QGAN on SAT4 the optimal capacity of the classical discriminator (and of a classical generator) scales exponentially with the capacity of the quantum generator. — [arXiv:2601.05036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05036)
- MosaiQ's successor literature and the 2022 to 2024 patch QGANs are the main alternatives; the latent approach is also the design behind the quantum-prior family in KQ3 (Rudolph 2022, Wilson 2021, Bacarreza 2025).

### Inferences
- The strongest 2024 to 2026 trend is to move the quantum generator into a learned latent space (classical AE/VAE) and use Wasserstein losses; this is how every recent "practical" result (LaSt-QGAN, LaSt-QWGAN, VAE-QWGAN, LatentQGAN) reaches realistic data sizes with 5 to 15 qubits.
- The 2026 single-circuit end-to-end image QGANs (ReQGAN, CoQui, BasicQGAN) are a reaction to the resource scaling and loss of global structure of patch methods; they are simulation-only so far.

### Gaps
- No "entropic QGAN" paper and no tensor-network-generator QGAN were found; both terms in the assignment may refer to work I could not locate under those names.
- The VAE-QWGAN author list beyond the first author was not captured by the API query used (the API returned "Aaron Mark Thomas et al., 3 authors").

## KQ3. Quantum VAEs and quantum autoencoders relevant to generation

### Takeaway
Three distinct lineages carry the "quantum autoencoder/VAE" label: (1) Romero-Olson-Aspuru-Guzik's quantum autoencoder for compressing quantum states (and its enhanced, denoising, mixed-reference and disentangling descendants, mostly non-generative); (2) the D-Wave QVAE lineage in which a classical VAE's latent prior is a (quantum) Boltzmann machine sampled by an annealer (Khoshaman 2018, Vinci 2020, Rocutto 2021, Gircha 2023, Lu 2024, Kim and Park 2026); and (3) gate-model hybrid or fully quantum VAEs for images, molecules and text (SQ-VAE 2022, zeta-QVAE 2024/2025, Q-VAE 2025, QINR-VAE 2026, disentangled QVAE 2026), plus a 2024 to 2026 "quantum latent distribution" line that uses photonic samplers or PQCs as the latent prior of a classical generator.

## KQ5. Reported results and known limitations

### Takeaway
Reported positives are parameter efficiency (two to three orders of magnitude fewer generator parameters at comparable quality) and a few hardware-level wins against matched classical baselines; the documented limitations are mode collapse (now addressed by VAE priors, InfoGAN-style terms and Wasserstein losses), barren plateaus and loss concentration (especially for explicit losses), generalisation failure of fully quantum image QGANs, qubit-driven resolution limits that force patching, PCA or latent compression, and thin evaluation (single hardware runs, noiseless simulators).

### Cited Findings
Positive results and classical comparisons
- PQWGAN: 28 x 28 images without dimensionality reduction, comparable to classical frameworks with three orders of magnitude fewer trainable generator parameters. — [arXiv:2212.11614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11614)
- Riofrío et al.: quantum models need similar or fewer parameters than classical counterparts, up to two orders of magnitude fewer. — [arXiv:2301.09363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09363)

### Inferences
- Nearly every claimed advantage is parameter-count efficiency at matched quality, which is not the same as wall-clock or sample-quality advantage; Frkatovic 2025 and the split-head ablation are the most careful negative or neutral controls so far.
- The mode-collapse remedies converging on VAE-style data-dependent priors suggest the practical architecture for 2026 is "classical VAE latent + small quantum Wasserstein generator".

### Gaps
- No published head-to-head benchmark across more than two hardware vendors with repeated runs was found.
