# Quantum generative AI converges on hybrid latents

As of November 2026, quantum generative AI is a field of eight architecture families whose credible results share one recipe: a small quantum sampler, meaning **2 to 20 gate-model qubits, 16 photons, or up to 2,000 annealer spins**, placed in the latent space or at the input of an otherwise classical generator, with the quantum part never larger than a classical simulator can check. Quantum GANs, quantum VAEs and autoencoders, quantum diffusion and flow models, quantum circuit Born machines, quantum Boltzmann machines, tensor-network generators and quantum transformers all exist in the literature, but direct quantum generation of classical data is still at MNIST scale, every hardware run is an inference-only feasibility check, and the most careful 2025 to 2026 controls (parameter-matched classical baselines, architecture-matched ablations, tuned classical priors) remove most of the claimed gains. Theory settled the terms in the same period: generative models that anticoncentrate enough to carry sampling hardness are untrainable on average, trainable variational models are classically surrogatable at small scale, and the routes to advantage that survive are quantum-origin data, provable expressivity resources such as contextuality, and samplers that are trained classically and only deployed on quantum hardware. AI venues carry a thin generative strand inside a growing quantum presence: roughly one main-track quantum generative paper per year until 2025 and about six across ICML and NeurIPS 2026, against **45 quantum-titled NeurIPS 2026 papers**, while computer vision has exactly one quantum generative main-track paper (MosaiQ, ICCV 2023) and the SIGGRAPH family has one quantum technical paper and no TOG paper. The two reference papers sit on opposite sides of the quantum-prior line: Xiao et al. train a programmable-circuit prior jointly with a pretrained BigGAN and compare against an untrained classical latent, whereas Bacarreza et al. freeze a boson-sampling latent from ORCA's PT-2 processor and control for everything except photon interference, which is why the latter reached ICML 2026 and why neither answers whether a trained quantum prior beats a trained classical prior of equal cost. For a fluids and numerics researcher with two shared RTX 4090s, the realistic work is simulator-first: controlled latent-distribution benchmarks that add the controls both papers lack, fluid-field datasets with ground truth from the user's own solvers, and end-to-end resource costing (shots, samples, wall-clock) that the literature does not report.

## Eight families exist, and every scaled result hides a classical pipeline

The architectures asked about are all real and all documented, but they differ sharply in maturity, and a single pattern explains the scaling behaviour of every one of them: whenever a quantum generative model produces anything larger than a few-pixel image or a few correlated variables, a classical autoencoder, PCA stage, patch decomposition or full classical generator is doing the heavy lifting, and the quantum circuit is a prior, a latent denoiser, or an input layer of a few qubits. The subsections below take the families in the order the question lists them; the table at the end of the section summarises them.

### Quantum GANs moved from patches to classical latent spaces

The quantum GAN literature begins with two companion April 2018 preprints. Lloyd and Weedbrook defined quantum adversarial games for quantum or classical data, proved that the unique fixed point is the generator reproducing the data statistics, and argued for a possible exponential advantage only when the data are measurement samples on high-dimensional spaces ([arXiv:1804.09139](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09139)); Dallaire-Demers and Killoran gave the circuit constructions and quantum gradient estimation that made the idea trainable ([arXiv:1804.08641](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08641)). Within a year the field split into a quantum-data lineage that learns states (Benedetti et al., [arXiv:1806.00463](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00463); the first superconducting proof of principle by Hu et al., which reached **98.8 percent fidelity** replicating a qubit channel, [arXiv:1808.02893](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02893); Google's entangling EQ-GAN on Sycamore, [arXiv:2105.00080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00080)) and a hybrid lineage with a quantum generator and a classical discriminator for classical distributions (Zeng et al., [arXiv:1808.03425](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03425); Situ et al., [arXiv:1807.01235](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01235)). Zoufal, Lucchi and Woerner turned the hybrid design into the de facto reference implementation by framing it as approximate distribution loading into an n-qubit state with polynomially many gates, implemented in Qiskit and run on IBM processors ([arXiv:1904.00043](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00043)). The first image-generation experiment followed in 2021, when Huang et al. generated handwritten digits on a superconducting processor with a patch-wise sub-generator scheme and benchmarked against MLP and CNN GANs at similar parameter counts ([arXiv:2010.06201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06201)).

The variants that followed are best read as successive answers to the resolution problem. Patch-based image QGANs culminated in PQWGAN, which generated **28 x 28 MNIST and Fashion-MNIST images without dimensionality reduction with three orders of magnitude fewer trainable generator parameters** than classical counterparts, in simulation ([arXiv:2212.11614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11614)), and in MosaiQ, the only quantum generative model in any CVPR, ICCV, ECCV or WACV main track, run on IBM Jakarta and PennyLane simulators ([arXiv:2308.11096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11096)). Patching captures only local structure, which is why the 2024 to 2026 work moved the quantum generator into a learned latent space: LaSt-QGAN at CERN trains a style-based hybrid generator inside a classical autoencoder latent on Fashion-MNIST and SAT4 with **10 qubits** ([arXiv:2406.02668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02668)); VAE-QWGAN fuses a VAE decoder with a quantum Wasserstein generator and samples a Gaussian-mixture prior to cut mode collapse ([arXiv:2409.10339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10339)); and LaSt-QWGAN for drug design reports a **110-parameter quantum generator statistically comparable to a roughly 700,000-parameter MOSES latent GAN**, with circuits up to 15 qubits in simulation and an inference-only run on an IBM Heron device with 5 qubits sampling 2,500 molecules ([arXiv:2603.22399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22399)). Style-based generators that re-upload noise at every rotation (Bravo-Prieto et al. for LHC events, deployed on trapped-ion and superconducting hardware, [arXiv:2110.06933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06933)) and conditional generators (Certo et al. for option pricing, [arXiv:2304.10382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10382); Pham et al. with Fire Opal error suppression improving KL divergence by 30 to 40 percent on IBM hardware, [arXiv:2406.09341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09341)) complete the practical toolkit. A 2026 series by Yang et al. reacts against patching with single-circuit end-to-end image synthesis (ReQGAN, [arXiv:2603.18554](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18554); CoQui, which reads each pixel as the expectation of a colour qubit queried at spatial coordinates, [arXiv:2608.11884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11884)), but these are simulation-only so far. Photonic QGANs exist on silicon chips ([arXiv:2310.00585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00585); [arXiv:2404.05921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05921)) and on Quandela's single-photon processor, where a Fock-encoded linear-optical generator was trained end to end for images ([arXiv:2405.06023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06023)); ReCon is the first QGAN on analog Rydberg arrays and claims 33 percent better FID than the superconducting state of the art ([arXiv:2408.13389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13389)). The notes found no QGAN whose generator is a tensor network and no paper calling itself an entropic QGAN; the quantum Wasserstein strand runs through Chakrabarti et al. at NeurIPS 2019 ([arXiv:1911.00111](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00111)) and the quantum earth mover's distance of Kiani et al. ([arXiv:2101.03037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03037)).

The documented limitations matter more than the variants. Mode collapse is attributed to uninformed priors and is now addressed by VAE-style data-dependent priors and Wasserstein losses ([arXiv:2409.10339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10339); InfoQGAN, [arXiv:2309.01363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01363)). Explicit losses such as KL produce "a new flavour of barren plateaus" and the implicit MMD loss is provably trainable only with low-bodied kernels that cannot see high-order correlations ([arXiv:2305.02881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02881)). Frkatovic et al. showed that fully quantum image QGANs with pure-state generators converge to the average of the training data, with a fidelity lower bound explaining why ([arXiv:2508.09844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09844)), and Li et al. estimated that a fully quantum GAN would need more than 90 qubits even for QM9-sized molecules ([arXiv:2101.03438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03438)). The one recent claim pointing the other way is Liepelt and Baglio's observation that the classical discriminator capacity needed to match a latent style-based quantum generator scales exponentially with the quantum generator's capacity, which they call a hint of advantage but obtained only after careful autoencoder tuning ([arXiv:2601.05036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05036)). Nearly every positive claim in this family is parameter-count efficiency at matched quality, which is not a wall-clock or sample-quality advantage.

### Quantum VAEs split into annealer priors and gate-model encoders

Three distinct things carry the "quantum autoencoder or VAE" label. The original quantum autoencoder of Romero, Olson and Aspuru-Guzik compresses datasets of quantum states ([arXiv:1612.02806](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02806)); its descendants (denoising, enhanced-feature, mixed-reference, disentangling, and the photonic qutrit-to-qubit experiment of Pepper et al., [arXiv:1810.01637](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01637)) are mostly non-generative compression tools, and a 2025 addition makes them generative only by training a QGAN on the latent ([arXiv:2509.16186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16186)). The D-Wave lineage is the generative one: Khoshaman et al. defined the quantum variational autoencoder as a VAE whose discrete latent prior is a quantum Boltzmann machine trained with a quantum lower bound on the ELBO ([arXiv:1802.05779](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05779)), Vinci et al. trained it end to end with a D-Wave 2000Q as the Boltzmann sampler and reached competitive MNIST performance ([arXiv:1912.02119](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02119)), and the line has grown steadily through Gircha et al.'s discrete VAE generating 2,331 novel ChEMBL-like molecules ([arXiv:2108.11644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11644)) and the TRIUMF calorimeter surrogates ([arXiv:2412.04677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04677)) to two 2026 results: Kim and Park trained VAEs with general Boltzmann priors using **up to 2,000 qubits on a D-Wave Advantage2** for MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and CelebA, with faster convergence and lower reconstruction loss than a Gaussian-prior VAE of identical encoder-decoder architecture ([arXiv:2604.00919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00919)), and Kunugi et al. with Shionogi put an annealer-sampled Boltzmann prior into a transformer molecular generator and obtained **97 percent chemically valid molecules versus 73 percent for the same architecture with classical MCMC sampling** ([arXiv:2602.15451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15451); [doi:10.1038/s41598-026-49186-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-49186-8)). The third strand is gate-model hybrid or fully quantum VAEs for classical data: zeta-QVAE is a fully quantum VAE with regularised mixed-state latents published in Physical Review A ([arXiv:2402.17749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17749)), Q-VAE puts quantum encoding inside the encoder and reports lower FID than classical VAEs on MNIST and USPS ([arXiv:2501.06259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06259)), QINR-VAE uses a quantum implicit-neural-representation decoder and argues QGANs "suffer from the low diversity problem" ([arXiv:2603.06755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06755)), a 2026 paper shows individual qubits acting as semantically interpretable latent factors ([arXiv:2610.07196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07196)), and QVAE-Mole for 3D molecule generation is one of the very few quantum generative papers in a NeurIPS main track ([NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/286e7ab0ce6a68282394c92361c27b57-Abstract-Conference.html)). The 3D-QAE at BMVC 2023 is a fully quantum autoencoder for point clouds trained in noiseless PennyLane simulation ([arXiv:2311.05604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05604)). The honest reading of the annealer results is that they are quantum-assisted sampling for classical energy-based models: the strongest 2026 comparisons are against a Gaussian-prior VAE or a classical MCMC sampler of the same architecture, not against a tuned classical Boltzmann sampler, and earlier annealer studies found only modest differences from MCMC ([arXiv:2508.15697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15697)) or contrastive divergence with better learning curves ([arXiv:2005.03247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03247)).

### Quantum diffusion and flows remain 4 to 12 qubit feasibility studies

Quantum diffusion was founded by five 2023 to 2024 papers along one dividing line: diffusion over classical data with a parametrised-circuit denoiser versus diffusion over ensembles of quantum states. Parigi, Martina and Caruso proposed quantum-noise-driven diffusion, arguing NISQ noise should be a resource and demonstrating a 4-qubit, 256-layer denoiser on two-dimensional toy data ([arXiv:2308.12013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12013)). QuDDPM, in Physical Review Letters, scrambles quantum-state ensembles toward Haar-random states and denoises with circuits plus ancilla measurement; all results are classical simulations with **1 to 4 data qubits** and the generalisation scaling is shown numerically with the proof left open ([arXiv:2310.05866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05866)). Cacioppo et al. built full, latent and conditioned variants on MNIST, reported FID 38.2 for the conditioned latent model with no numerical classical baseline, and ran a 4-qubit inference on ibm_hanoi ([arXiv:2311.15444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15444)); their IJCNN 2025 follow-up states that "our results do not surpass classical diffusion models in the generation of classical samples" ([doi:10.1109/IJCNN64981.2025.11227283](https://doi.org/10.1109/IJCNN64981.2025.11227283)). Koelle et al. (with Ommer) introduced Q-Dense and QU-Net denoisers, claimed **MNIST 8 x 8 FID around 100, about 20 points better than classical models at roughly 1,000 parameters**, ran a one-step unitary sampler on a 7-qubit IBM device, and also reported mode collapse on CIFAR-10 and that classical models keep the edge on inpainting ([arXiv:2401.07049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07049)). QGDM, a fully channel-theoretic model for quantum states, reached mixed-state fidelity 0.992 against 0.477 for QuGAN and was accepted in IEEE TPAMI, the first top-tier ML journal placement in the family ([arXiv:2401.07039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07039)). The latent-space image variant of De Falco et al. uses a 10-qubit denoiser in a 10-dimensional autoencoder latent and reports **FID 40.40 versus 44.36 on MNIST and 20.11 versus 30.56 on EuroSAT against a 330-parameter classical model**, with the advantage persisting when training on 20 percent of the data, all in noiseless simulation ([arXiv:2501.11174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11174)). Mixed-state QuDDPM removes the need for high-fidelity scrambling unitaries ([arXiv:2411.17608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17608)), and a 2026 paper proves that QuDDPM suffers a barren plateau of a new kind as system size grows and proposes a trainable architecture ([arXiv:2512.06695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06695)).

Hardware results across the whole diffusion and flow family are inference-only with 4 to 12 qubits and are consistently worse than simulation. Parigi et al.'s quantum-walk forward process on ibm_brisbane with 4 qubits gives **FID 169 against 114 for the simulated model**, with a classical MLP as the denoiser ([arXiv:2505.22193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22193)); Venturelli et al. ran a discrete-time quantum walk forward process on 6 to 7 qubits of ibm_torino for medical images with a classical U-Net backward and call it a proof of concept ([arXiv:2609.31070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31070)); Quantum MeanFlow executed 10 to 11 qubit flow-matching circuits on IBM Heron devices and recovered hardware accuracy from 0.30 to 0.80 only with best-of-8 rejection sampling ([arXiv:2609.02186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02186)); a hybrid denoising transformer with quantum feed-forward blocks ran on IQM Emerald with 128 shots per expectation value ([arXiv:2606.27561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27561)). The two most careful 2026 studies are negative: Kim and Yoo find that parameter-matched classical controls match quantum cores on FID across DDPM and latent diffusion, so "the experiments do not establish a quantum parameter-efficiency advantage", and they diagnose a phase-aliasing failure of angle embeddings under score targets proportional to 1/sigma ([arXiv:2607.09108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09108)); Tran et al.'s fully quantum latent diffusion lowers denoising infidelity but yields downstream macro-F1 of 0.11 against 0.44 for a comparably sized classical model, with post-selection accepting 3 x 10^-7 of runs ([arXiv:2610.38974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38974)). Quantum normalizing flows exist in two forms, a unitary used directly as the bijection with no log-determinant (Rosenhahn and Hirche, anomaly detection on 4 to 5 qubit UCI data, [arXiv:2402.02866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02866)) and PQCs embedded in classical coupling layers (HQCNF, FID 1.77 against 4.80 for a hybrid QGAN on MNIST digits 0 and 1 with no classical-flow baseline, [arXiv:2405.13808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13808)); neither delivers a density estimate with a proper change-of-variables likelihood on a quantum circuit. Quantum flow matching is a circuit-level propagator between density matrices rather than a learned velocity field, published in PRX Intelligence and simulation-only ([arXiv:2508.12413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12413)), with a conditional variant whose gain over QuDDPM is matched by a class-conditional Gaussian at the main operating point ([arXiv:2609.14019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14019)). The only strand with asymptotic complexity claims is quantum algorithms that accelerate classical samplers: quantum ODE solvers for diffusion probabilistic models ([arXiv:2502.14252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14252)), Wavefunction Flows, which maps continuous flow models to a Schroedinger equation and is in the NeurIPS 2026 main track ([arXiv:2510.08462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08462)), and QD3PM, which proves linear KL scaling for factorised discrete diffusion and learns the joint posterior in Hilbert space ([arXiv:2505.05151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05151)). No quantum diffusion or flow paper has appeared in a NeurIPS, ICML or ICLR main track except Wavefunction Flows, and no one in the family reports wall-clock or energy comparisons against a classical GPU.

### Born machines became the field's theory and advantage workhorse

The quantum circuit Born machine, proposed twice in 2018 as data-driven quantum circuit learning with a clipped-KL cost ([arXiv:1801.07686](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07686)) and as the MMD-trained QCBM ([arXiv:1804.04168](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04168)), is the family where the serious theory lives. Coyle et al. proved that Ising Born machine outputs cannot be sampled classically under standard complexity assumptions and introduced Sinkhorn and Stein training ([arXiv:1904.02214](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02214)); Zhu et al. trained one on a trapped-ion computer in 2019 ([arXiv:1812.08862](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08862)); Gili et al. defined the generalisation vocabulary (fidelity, rate, coverage, utility) now expected of advantage claims and showed QCBMs can emit valid novel samples, beating a GAN in the data-limited regime ([arXiv:2201.08770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08770); [arXiv:2207.13645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13645)); and Hibat-Allah et al. ran the first quantitative race against transformers, RNNs, VAEs and WGANs on a 20-variable task, finding QCBMs more data-efficient only when data are scarce ([arXiv:2303.15626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15626)). The hardness side is equally developed: Sweke et al. proved a quantum-classical separation for PAC distribution learning ([arXiv:2007.14451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14451)), Hinsche et al. showed a single T gate makes distribution learning hard ([arXiv:2207.03140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03140)), and Nietner et al. proved average-case statistical-query hardness for brickwork circuit outputs ([arXiv:2305.05765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05765)), which caps any learning advantage for typical Born distributions because MMD, sampled KL and discriminator losses are all statistical queries.

The dominant 2025 to 2026 direction is "train classically, deploy on quantum": instantaneous quantum polynomial (IQP) Born machines whose MMD loss and gradients are classically computable while sampling stays hard. Recio-Armengol, Eisert and Meyer scaled this to **1,000 simulated qubits** ([arXiv:2503.02934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02934)); Kurkin et al. proved universality with ancillas ([arXiv:2510.08476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08476)); Shen et al.'s closed-form gradient variances are in the NeurIPS 2026 main track ([arXiv:2602.11042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11042)); Lerch et al. proved barren plateaus for full-range random initialisation and gave variance bounds for identity and data-agnostic initialisations ([arXiv:2603.14576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14576)); Balló-Gimbernat et al. sampled classically trained IQP circuits on IBM's Aachen processor from **28 to 153 qubits** to generate graphs ([arXiv:2511.05267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05267)); and Ryu et al. decomposed a **316-qubit IQP circuit onto 156-qubit IBM Heron chips** via noise injection ([arXiv:2609.35163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35163)). The same paradigm has been extended to boson sampling ([arXiv:2603.11014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11014)), fermion sampling ([arXiv:2511.13844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13844)) and spectral (group-Fourier) Born machines ([arXiv:2607.06675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06675)), while the Born Ultimatum identifies QCBMs as quantum Fourier models and quantifies when tensor-network or Pauli-propagation surrogates dequantise them ([arXiv:2511.01845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01845)). The most prominent advantage claim is Google's September 2025 preprint demonstrating provably trainable, barren-plateau-free, classically hard generative families on a **68-qubit superconducting processor** for learning classically intractable distributions and learning circuits for simulation ([arXiv:2509.09033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09033)); it has no peer-reviewed version or independent replication yet. The applied highlights are a 16-qubit IBM-trained QCBM inside a molecular generator that produced synthesised and tested KRAS inhibitor candidates, published in Nature Biotechnology ([arXiv:2402.08210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08210); [doi:10.1038/s41587-024-02526-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-024-02526-3)), and IonQ's 2026 SAR change-detection result, where a QCBM in copula space trained and run on an IonQ Forte with 20-qubit circuits reached filtered F1 0.32 against 0.16 and 0.24 for two classical baselines on heavy-tailed radar data, with parity on Gaussianised data ([arXiv:2609.05313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05313)).

### Quantum Boltzmann machines found trainable subclasses and lost the RBM argument

Amin et al. defined the QBM as a Gibbs state of a transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian trained on a bound of the log-likelihood ([arXiv:1601.02036](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02036)) and Kieferová and Wiebe gave relative-entropy training on quantum data ([arXiv:1612.05204](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05204)); gate-model training arrived with Zoufal et al.'s variational QBMs prepared by imaginary-time evolution ([arXiv:2006.06004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06004)). The revival since 2023 is rigorous rather than empirical: Coopmans and Benedetti proved that fully visible QBMs trained with relative entropy have a convex loss and polynomial sample complexity ([arXiv:2306.14969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14969)); Patel and Wilde derived natural-gradient training ([arXiv:2410.24058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24058)); Minervini, Patel and Wilde introduced evolved QBMs ([arXiv:2501.03367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03367)); and Wilde's December 2025 papers give the first general Born-rule generative training recipe for evolved QBMs via the Donsker-Varadhan representation, noting that "efficient training methods have remained elusive" for a decade, and name a fully quantum contrastive-divergence algorithm as the main open problem ([arXiv:2512.02721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02721); [arXiv:2512.19819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19819)). Two expressivity results bracket the family: low-dimensional fully visible QBMs can learn distributions normally associated with higher-dimensional systems ([arXiv:2410.16363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16363)), but Demidik et al. proved that quantum restricted Boltzmann machines are expressively equivalent to classical RBMs, a negative result for the most common proposal ([arXiv:2502.17562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17562)). No gate-model hardware demonstration of a QBM trained for generation beyond a handful of qubits was found; on annealers, diabatic annealing with schedule-to-temperature control gave faster RBM convergence than classical sampling in Physical Review E ([arXiv:2509.09374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09374)) and a 120 x 120 quantum RBM on D-Wave Pegasus generated 1.6 million synthetic intrusion records ([arXiv:2502.03086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03086)).

### Tensor networks serve as baseline, initialiser and theory sandbox

Tensor-network Born machines are the quantum-inspired classical family and play three roles. Han et al.'s MPS Born machine with exact likelihoods, DMRG-style training and direct sampling ([arXiv:1709.01662](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01662)), tree tensor networks ([arXiv:1901.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02217)) and Glasser et al.'s NeurIPS 2019 proof that locally purified states are strictly more expressive than MPS and Born machines at fixed bond dimension, with 2-local circuits mapped to LPS ([arXiv:1907.03741](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03741)), make them the strongest quantum-inspired baseline, used as such in the Zapata race ([arXiv:2303.15626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15626)) and in generator-enhanced optimisation published in Nature Communications ([arXiv:2101.06250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06250)). They are also the initialiser that avoids barren plateaus: Rudolph et al. decompose a tensor-network state into circuit parameters and show the learned initialisation improves training ([arXiv:2208.13673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13673)), building on Dborin et al. ([arXiv:2106.05742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05742)). And they are the simulator of record for quantum generators: Dechant et al. simulate a financial-time-series QGAN with MPS and study bond dimension as a hyperparameter ([arXiv:2507.22035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22035)), and Xiao et al.'s reference paper uses an MPS circuit of bond dimension 16 to emulate 120 qubits (see the reference-paper section). The 2025 to 2026 tensor-network papers are optimisation and structure-learning improvements (second-order methods, [arXiv:2501.18691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18691); adaptive tree structures from bond mutual information, [arXiv:2408.10669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10669); approximation-complexity theory for MPO Born machines, [arXiv:2605.11471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11471)), and a 2026 review covers the whole quantum-inspired strand ([arXiv:2604.14287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14287)); the quantum-circuit bridge has largely migrated into the IQP literature.

### Quantum transformers reach parity, not generation

Quantum self-attention began as a classifier (QSANN, [arXiv:2205.05625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05625)), and the field still has no hardware demonstration of autoregressive text generation on a quantum processor. The architecturally serious proposals are fault-tolerant: Guo et al.'s block-encoding algorithm for a full transformer inference pass including softmax attention ([arXiv:2402.16714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16714)), Liao and Ferrie's mapping of GPT onto circuits ([arXiv:2403.09418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09418)) and Quantinuum's Quixer built from linear combinations of unitaries and QSVT ([arXiv:2406.04305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04305)); quantum vision transformers appeared in Quantum after being withdrawn from ICLR 2023 ([arXiv:2209.08167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08167)). The variational ones are at parity on small tasks and their gains tend to vanish under capacity-matched classical controls, as Chen and Kuo's evaluation protocol and benchmarks show, with the classical transformer keeping the best perplexity (1.21) and BLEU-1 (0.2895) in their multi-model text-generation comparison ([arXiv:2504.05336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05336); [arXiv:2508.21332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21332)). Hybrid quantum LLM components arrived in late 2025 and 2026: HyQuT replaces about 10 percent of a 150M-parameter dialogue model's parameters with **10 qubits and 80 gates** at comparable quality ([arXiv:2511.10653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10653)), and Aizpurua et al. executed Cayley unitary adapters inserted into frozen projection layers of pre-trained LLMs on a **156-qubit IBM Quantum System Two, improving Llama 3.1 8B perplexity by 1.4 percent** ([arXiv:2605.05914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05914)). The only quantum transformers in AI main tracks are Quantum Doubly Stochastic Transformers at NeurIPS 2025 ([arXiv:2504.16275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16275)), a molecular-energy quantum transformer at AAAI 2026 ([doi:10.1609/aaai.v40i27.39407](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i27.39407)) and a sublinear-time quantum attention algorithm at ICLR 2026 ([arXiv:2602.00874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00874)), none of them generative. The genuinely quantum autoregressive generative line is instead the Born-machine sequence family: Gao et al.'s basis-enhanced Born machines are provably exponentially more expressive than classical models on sequence data ([arXiv:2101.08354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08354)), Anschuetz et al. trace an interpretable advantage to contextuality ([arXiv:2209.14353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14353)), and the 2026 coherent memory Born machine generates samples component by component from a data-size-independent register with exact training ([arXiv:2609.23050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23050)). Compositional QNLP reached text-level question answering on Quantinuum's H1-1 ([arXiv:2409.08777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08777)) but remains classification, and the "generative quantum AI" that Quantinuum markets is a classical transformer generating quantum circuits (GQE, [arXiv:2401.09253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09253); ADAPT-GQE with imipramine circuits executed on Helios, [arXiv:2607.22468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22468)).

## Hardware demonstrations top out at 20 trained qubits, 16 photons and 2,000 annealer spins

Applications cluster in six domains, and in each the question "on what hardware" has a short answer: the generator circuits that were actually trained on gate-model devices for classical data have 2 to about 20 qubits, larger qubit counts appear only through circuit replication or classical training with quantum deployment, Google's 68-qubit generative-advantage experiment on quantum-origin tasks is the one exception, and the inner-loop use of hardware at data sizes resembling current generative practice (CelebA, QM9) is confined to annealer priors and ORCA's photonic latents.

Image generation is the most crowded domain and the clearest illustration of the pattern. Every result at 28 x 28 or above relies on a classical autoencoder, PCA, patching or a classical generator: Rudolph et al.'s 28 x 28 digits come from a classical GAN whose prior is a 6 to 8 qubit QCBM trained on an IonQ trapped-ion processor ([arXiv:2012.03924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03924)), the CERN latent GANs run 10 qubits inside autoencoder latents ([arXiv:2406.02668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02668)), and the 2025 hardware image results are a 4-qubit forward process on ibm_brisbane ([arXiv:2505.22193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22193)) and a quantum input layer of a large classical Wasserstein GAN that generated 5-channel EBSD steel microstructure images end to end on an IonQ Aria-2, improving MMD over classical Bernoulli GANs in 70 percent of samples ([arXiv:2504.08728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08728)). Medical imaging supplies the first externally judged quality metric: a hybrid latent diffusion model with a quantum VAE for fundus images produced **85.9 percent gradable images against 68.9 percent for the classical model**, yet FID and CMMD still favoured the classical model and the quantum layers were tested only with an ibm_cleveland noise model ([arXiv:2508.09903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09903)). QIGL reported the lowest FID among classical and quantum baselines on knee X-rays with principal components and a Wasserstein loss ([arXiv:2406.13196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13196)), and a quanvolutional conditioned U-Net cut FID by 64 percent on EuroSAT in IEEE TGRS ([arXiv:2512.20448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20448)). No verified quantum generative result exists on CIFAR-10 beyond simulation, and none on CelebA or natural images without classical latent compression.

Molecules and drug discovery hold the strongest end-to-end stories, and both headline wins use the quantum device only as a sampler for a small prior inside a large classical generator. The KRAS study trained its 16-qubit QCBM on IBM hardware, synthesised 15 molecules and obtained experimentally confirmed binders ([arXiv:2402.08210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08210)); the Shionogi study on a D-Wave Advantage2 raised validity from 73 to 97 percent and the share of molecules with QED at least 0.7 from 43.15 to 66.79 percent ([arXiv:2602.15451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15451)); ORCA's PT-2 latents improved Frechet ChemNet Distance on QM9 ([arXiv:2508.19857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19857)); and a bioRxiv preprint seeds a peptide GAN with latents from a 32-mode photonic processor, raising the predicted yield of strong MHC-I binders across 131 HLA alleles with ELISA validation for three ([doi:10.64898/2026.07.09.736951](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.09.736951)). QM9 or small subsets are the de facto benchmark across QGAN-HG ([arXiv:2101.03438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03438)), Kao et al.'s quantum MolGAN variants ([arXiv:2210.16823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16823)), the Bayesian-optimised BO-QGAN with its 2.27x drug-candidate-score claim ([arXiv:2506.01177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01177)) and LaSt-QWGAN; metrics are validity, QED and similar property scores, and no paper except KRAS and the peptide preprint reports docking or wet-lab results.

Finance was the first real-data test bed and shows parity or small gains on 2 to 4 variable problems: qGAN distribution loading on IBM Q ([arXiv:1904.00043](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00043)), QCBM versus RBM on currency pairs with Rigetti runs ([arXiv:2008.00691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00691)), copulas as maximally entangled states trained on IonQ with up to 8 qubits ([arXiv:2109.06315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06315)) and the IonQ and GE Research risk-aggregation follow-up that observed decreasing training efficacy as models scale ([arXiv:2206.11937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11937)). The 2025 to 2026 finance and tabular work moves to larger IBM devices: LLM-designed hardware-aware QCBM ansaetze for Japanese government bond rates on 12 qubits ([arXiv:2509.08385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08385)), TabularQGAN benchmarked against CTGAN, CopulaGAN, VAE-GMM and an LLM in noiseless simulation ([arXiv:2505.22533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22533)), and an in-silico clinical-trial generator evaluated on the 156-qubit Heron r2 device ibm_basquecountry that beat TVAE, CTGAN and CopulaGAN on MAE and Jensen-Shannon fidelity for scarce data ([arXiv:2608.28168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28168)). Where hardware helps it is in heavy-tailed or strongly non-Gaussian dependence, which is also where IonQ's SAR result reports its gains.

High-energy physics has the most coherent institutional body of work (CERN openlab, DESY, ORNL, TRIUMF): style-qGANs for Monte Carlo events on two hardware types ([arXiv:2110.06933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06933)), conditional Born machines for muonic-force-carrier events on IBM hardware ([arXiv:2205.07674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07674)), the dual-PQC calorimeter GAN under noise and on hardware ([arXiv:2205.15003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15003)), a Quantum Angle Generator whose hardware-trained model learns device noise characteristics ([arXiv:2307.05253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05253)), and the CaloQVAE line whose annealer-sampled priors are the only approach touching realistic shower dimensionality ([arXiv:2312.03179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03179); [arXiv:2410.22870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22870)). Performance is "comparable" to small classical models; no paper claims a speed or accuracy advantage over production fast-simulation tools.

Physics and engineering simulation, the domain closest to the reader, is small but has three genuine fluid-dynamics papers from 2025 to 2026. Ingelmann, Schindler and Schumacher encode the joint PDF of three velocity-fluctuation components in homogeneous turbulent shear flow in a parametric quantum circuit, draw Lagrangian tracer velocities as one-shot measurements, validate against a stochastic Lagrangian model and an MCMC sampler, and run up to **10 qubits on IQM's 20-qubit Resonance platform**, in Physics of Fluids ([arXiv:2506.14586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14586); [doi:10.1063/5.0285751](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0285751)). Hsain and Abbou compress GPU lattice-Boltzmann vorticity fields with a VQ-VAE into a 7-dimensional discrete latent and find that a QCBM and a QGAN produce samples with lower average minimum distance to the true latent distribution than an LSTM baseline, QCBM best, with no hardware and a weak baseline ([arXiv:2512.22672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22672)). Wang, Xue, Gao and Coveney supply an offline-trained quantum generative "Q-Prior" to a classical autoregressive predictor on Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, 2D Kolmogorov flow and 3D turbulent channel inflow, report up to **17.25 percent better predictive-distribution accuracy and 29.36 percent better full-spectrum fidelity**, trained the channel-inflow prior on a superconducting processor, and found the classical model without it became unstable, in Science Advances ([arXiv:2507.19861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19861)). Adjacent work puts variational circuits at the bottleneck of a corrective diffusion model for 10 m wind downscaling with gains that did not transfer uniformly to an out-of-distribution year ([arXiv:2605.23403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23403)), Rigetti's synthetic weather radar ([arXiv:2111.15605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15605)), Pasqal's differentiable quantum generative modelling that samples SDE solutions as quantile functions ([arXiv:2202.08253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08253); [arXiv:2108.03190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03190)) and quantum-enhanced MCMC for Ising Boltzmann distributions in Nature ([arXiv:2203.12497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12497)). There is no quantum generative PDE surrogate, no quantum diffusion-based flow super-resolution and nothing combining a quantum generative model with SPH, particle methods or mesh-free simulation beyond Lagrangian tracer statistics.

Quantum-data applications are where "generative" and "hardware" meet at scale: Google's 68-qubit experiment ([arXiv:2509.09033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09033)), EQ-GAN on Sycamore preparing an approximate QRAM ([arXiv:2105.00080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00080)), QuDDPM and its descendants for state ensembles, and a 2026 universality theorem for the many-body projected ensemble ([arXiv:2601.18637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18637)). The classical-generative-AI-for-quantum direction (transformer QEC decoders, [arXiv:2503.21374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21374); GQE circuit generation) is where industrial announcements concentrate. Across all domains, company press releases quote the most favourable number: IonQ's release cites F1 0.41 for the SAR task where the paper's abstract says 0.32 ([IonQ press release](https://www.ionq.com/news/ionq-demonstrates-quantum-generative-modeling-for-high-resolution-radar-change-detection); [arXiv:2609.05313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05313)), and Quantinuum's blog describes ADAPT-GQE as about one order of magnitude faster than ADAPT-VQE while trade press quotes three to four orders ([Quantinuum blog](https://www.quantinuum.com/blog/quantinuum-and-nvidia-validate-generative-quantum-ai-framework-for-pharmaceutical-r-d)). No independent third-party replication of any company-reported generative advantage exists.

## Theory now says where advantage can and cannot come from

The reason the empirical landscape looks the way it does became clear in 2025 and 2026, when several theory strands converged on one message: a generic parametrised circuit trained on a hard classical distribution cannot be the source of a generative advantage. The trainability strand started with barren plateaus ([arXiv:1803.11173](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11173)), was unified in a 2025 Nature Reviews Physics review ([arXiv:2405.00781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00781)), and was sharpened for generative losses by Rudolph et al., who showed that explicit losses concentrate exponentially because model probabilities are exponentially small regardless of depth, while MMD with broad kernels is trainable but blind to high-order correlations ([arXiv:2305.02881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02881)); Letcher, Woerner and Zoufal added design-free, classically estimable gradient bounds and proved that a suitably designed discriminator makes hybrid qGANs barren-plateau free ([arXiv:2309.12681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12681)), and Anschuetz and Kiani showed that underparametrised landscapes are swamped with poor local minima ([arXiv:2205.05786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05786)). The dequantisation strand then closed the other door: data re-uploading models admit classical surrogates after training ([arXiv:2206.11740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11740)), the structures that guarantee absence of barren plateaus generically enable classical simulation after a data-acquisition phase ([arXiv:2312.09121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09121)), quantum convolutional networks are effectively classically simulable ([arXiv:2408.12739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12739)), Pauli propagation estimates observables of random circuits at any depth ([arXiv:2409.01706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01706)), and Gil-Fuster et al. at ICLR 2025 showed that trainable yet non-dequantisable models can exist but require deliberate construction ([arXiv:2406.07072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07072)). Herbst, Brandic and Perez-Salinas made the generative consequence explicit: models whose outputs anticoncentrate, which is the property every sampling-hardness proof for random circuits, IQP and boson sampling relies on, are untrainable on average because essentially all losses concentrate, while the sparse-output cases that restore trainability invite classical surrogate sampling ([arXiv:2512.24801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24801)). **A quantum generative model can therefore be hard to sample or trainable from data, but not both by generic means.**

Three routes survive this squeeze. The first is quantum-origin data and structured tasks, which Google's generative-advantage families exploit by construction, circumventing the Hinsche and Nietner hardness results ([arXiv:2509.09033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09033)), and which also underlies Lloyd and Weedbrook's original advantage argument. The second is a provable expressivity resource: Gao et al.'s separation between quantum-extended and classical Bayesian networks rests on nonlocality and contextuality rather than complexity conjectures ([arXiv:2101.08354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08354)), and Anschuetz has since shown arbitrary polynomial separations in trainable sequence models ([arXiv:2402.08606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08606)). The third is to use the quantum device only as a sampler whose training signal is classically computable, the IQP, boson-sampling, fermionic and spectral Born machines above, or to use no training on the quantum side at all, which is exactly the design of the ORCA reference paper. Expressivity universality results cut the other way: expectation-value-sampling circuits are universal generative models with tight qubit bounds, but universality is shared with classical networks and so supports feasibility rather than advantage ([arXiv:2402.09848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09848)), and generic circuits lack backpropagation scaling, so gradient cost grows with parameter count ([arXiv:2306.14962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14962)).

The benchmarking literature has moved in step. Schuld and Killoran argued the field should stop organising itself around speedups ([arXiv:2203.01340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01340)); Bowles, Ahmed and Schuld benchmarked about a dozen QML models against tuned classical baselines and found no systematic quantum edge, with a repository that now ships a generative template ([arXiv:2403.07059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07059)); Raj, Mathur and Perdomo-Ortiz benchmarked thirteen quantum and classical generative models and showed that a converged MMD loss does not predict coverage of unseen valid samples, so "train classical, deploy quantum" requires rethinking generalisation ([arXiv:2608.31117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31117)); and QUARK gained a generative module with novelty and validity metrics and a scaling and noise study ([arXiv:2308.04082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04082); [arXiv:2403.18662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18662)). Two position papers at the 2026 AI venues state the community's verdict: "Quantum Deep Learning Still Needs a Quantum Leap" in the ICML 2026 position track ([PMLR v306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/gundlach26a.html)) and "Stop Quantum Machine Learning; do AI-for-Quantum instead" at NeurIPS 2026 ([NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/140038)).

For photonic latents specifically, a further literature bears on what hardware-scale results can mean. Boson sampling is hard to sample exactly and approximately under permanent conjectures in the dilute regime ([arXiv:1011.3245](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3245)), but its outputs are efficiently distinguishable from uniform by cheap row-norm statistics, so part of the latent's structure is classically accessible ([arXiv:1309.7460](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7460)); lossy, finite-size experiments are approximated classically as well as or better than the hardware matches the ideal distribution ([arXiv:2306.03709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03709); [arXiv:2109.11525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11525)), loss thresholds for efficient simulation are known ([arXiv:1801.06166](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06166); [arXiv:1712.10037](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.10037)), and ORCA's own causal-cone simulation of loop-based interferometers estimates the scale needed for advantage ([arXiv:2411.16873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16873)). Current 16-photon latents therefore sit where classical imitations with matched low-order statistics exist, which is why a spoofer baseline is the missing control discussed below.

## AI venues publish quantum theory and AI-for-quantum, rarely quantum generation

Quantum computing has become a steady, small presence at the machine-learning venues, but quantum generative modelling is a sliver of it. Counting quantum-titled main-track acceptances from the official proceedings, NeurIPS went **2, 5, 3, 5, 10, 14, 13, 15 and 45 papers from 2018 to 2026**, ICML 0, 1, 4, 3, 4, 16, 14, 17 and 23, and ICLR 1, 0, 2, 0, 1, 4, 7, 14 and 10, with AAAI at roughly 2 to 15 per year but heavily diluted by quantum-inspired NLP ([NeurIPS 2026 program](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/papers.html?filter=titles); [PMLR v306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/); [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/)). Even the record year is marginal in share: 45 of 9,130 NeurIPS 2026 papers is about 0.5 percent, and 23 of 6,552 at ICML 2026 is 0.35 percent. Submission pressure is far higher than acceptance: at ICLR 2026, 11 quantum-titled papers were accepted against 47 rejected, 27 withdrawn and 11 desk-rejected, and 182 quantum-matching submissions are under review for ICLR 2027 ([OpenReview search](https://api2.openreview.net/notes/search?term=quantum&type=terms&content=all&source=forum&group=all&limit=5)). Thematically the accepted papers split into quantum algorithms with provable speedups for optimisation, games, bandits, sampling and reinforcement learning (the most prolific cluster), variational QML theory (barren plateaus, loss landscapes, generalisation, dequantisation, kernels), AI for quantum (architecture search, error-correction decoders, control, circuit synthesis, benchmarks such as QAS-Bench, QEM-Bench and QCircuitBench), and a thin generative strand; COLT has become the home of quantum learning theory with 14 papers since 2022.

The complete list of genuinely generative quantum papers in these main tracks is short. Quantum Wasserstein GANs at NeurIPS 2019 ([arXiv:1911.00111](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00111)), Anschuetz's critical points in quantum generative models at ICLR 2022 ([arXiv:2109.06957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06957)), QVAE-Mole at NeurIPS 2024, Bacarreza et al.'s quantum latent distributions at ICML 2026 ([PMLR v306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/bacarreza26a.html)), and at NeurIPS 2026 the IQP Born machine trainability paper, Wavefunction Flows, BuresTomFlow (flow matching for posterior tomography), density-operator latent variable models and score-based variational inference via maximally mixed states ([NeurIPS 2026 IQP poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/154648); [NeurIPS 2026 Wavefunction Flows poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/154358)). Around them sit quantum sampling algorithms (log-concave sampling at NeurIPS 2022, [arXiv:2210.06539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06539); Gibbs and non-logconcave sampling at ICML 2024, [arXiv:2210.08104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08104), [arXiv:2310.11445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11445)), a quantum-assisted VAE with a D-Wave-sampled prior at KDD 2020 ([arXiv:2006.07680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07680)), quantum-inspired sequence models at AISTATS (hidden quantum Markov models, u-MPS, [arXiv:2003.01039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01039)), and classical generative models for quantum problems (QuaDiM at ICLR 2025; Q-Flow at ICML 2023, [arXiv:2302.12235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12235)). The canonical generative results the field cites, the 2018 QGAN papers, Born machines, the quantum Boltzmann machine, QuDDPM and Gao et al.'s quantum-correlation enhancement, were all published in Physical Review X, PRL, PRA and npj Quantum Information, so a reference list for the area necessarily mixes physics journals with the ML-venue papers. Workshops carry more: the NeurIPS Machine Learning and the Physical Sciences workshop has had quantum generative items almost every year (CV-QGAN detectors 2020, quantum-enhanced VAEs 2023, Calo4pQVAE 2024, chaotic quantum diffusion and QBMs for RL 2025), NeurIPS 2024's MLNCP workshop hosted the quark-gluon quantum diffusion model ([arXiv:2412.21082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21082)), ICLR 2023's Physics4ML hosted ORCA's precursor poster ([OpenReview emPnxmK5Jlp](https://openreview.net/forum?id=emPnxmK5Jlp)), ICML 2025 GenBio hosted the BO-QGAN drug-design paper ([arXiv:2506.01177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01177)), and NeurIPS 2026 hosts the first dedicated QML workshop at a big-three venue, SaTQuML on secure and trustworthy QML with 39 accepted papers ([satquml.github.io](https://satquml.github.io/)). The high-energy-physics strand (CERN, the Gleyzer and Matchev groups, TRIUMF) is where quantum generative models are actually run on data at these workshops.

Computer vision and graphics are a near-empty source. Across CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, WACV, 3DV, BMVC and ACCV there are **30 main-track papers from 2020 to 2026 that use quantum computing, and exactly one is generative**: MosaiQ at ICCV 2023 ([arXiv:2308.11096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11096)). CVPR alone ran 1, 1, 4, 2, 3, 2 and 2 per year from 2020 to 2026, and the composition is combinatorial optimisation on D-Wave annealers through 2023 (point-set correspondence, permutation synchronisation, shape matching, multi-object tracking, multi-model fitting, motion segmentation) shifting since 2024 toward gate-model and hybrid networks on simulators or IBM, IonQ and QuEra hardware (robust fitting on IonQ as an ECCV 2024 oral, [arXiv:2409.02006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02006); ResQ on QuEra Aquila at ICCV 2025, [arXiv:2506.21537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21537); QuCNet with hardware-only inference on IBM Torino and Fez at CVPR 2026). One network of groups around Golyanik at MPI Informatics with Siegen, Imperial and the Italian model-fitting groups produced most of this; Golyanik is a co-author on 14 of the 30 ([4dqv.mpi-inf.mpg.de](https://4dqv.mpi-inf.mpg.de/)). The nearest neighbours to generation are the fully quantum 3D point-cloud autoencoder at BMVC 2023 ([arXiv:2311.05604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05604)) and quantum implicit neural representations (Quantum Visual Fields at NeurIPS 2025, [arXiv:2508.10900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10900)). The QCVML workshops at CVPR 2023, ECCV 2024 and ECCV 2026 have no paper track, and the quantum diffusion work that reached a vision journal is a medical detector built on a quantum diffusion backbone in TPAMI ([doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2024.3430839](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2024.3430839)) alongside the TPAMI survey ([arXiv:2206.03066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03066)) and QGDM. In graphics, the first and only SIGGRAPH-family technical paper is Quantum Ray Marching at SIGGRAPH Asia 2023, light transport recast as a quantum random walk with amplitude estimation, simulated only ([doi:10.1145/3610548.3618151](https://doi.org/10.1145/3610548.3618151)), followed by a SIGGRAPH Asia 2024 technical communication on wave-function-collapse content generation with QAOA ([doi:10.1145/3681758.3698015](https://doi.org/10.1145/3681758.3698015)); TVCG published hybrid quantum ray tracing with a Grover-type quadratic query gain ([arXiv:2204.12797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12797)), Computer Graphics Forum published the quantum coin integration method run on IBM Q in 2020 ([arXiv:1910.00263](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00263)), IEEE CG&A ran a Quantum Visual Computing special issue in 2024 ([doi:10.1109/MCG.2024.3456850](https://doi.org/10.1109/MCG.2024.3456850)), and **ACM TOG and SGP have zero quantum papers**. For a reader interested in quantum generative models, the vision and graphics venues contribute MosaiQ, 3D-QAE and the TPAMI survey; the action is in quantum-computing venues and arXiv.

## The two reference papers bracket the quantum-prior line

Both papers in hand belong to one lineage: a quantum device supplies the latent distribution of an otherwise classical deep generative model. The lineage has three generations. In the first (2017 to 2021) an annealer or quantum Boltzmann machine supplies a trained prior over binary latents: Benedetti et al.'s quantum-assisted Helmholtz machine used **1,644 qubits of a D-Wave 2000Q** to model the binary code of a recognition network and is the first explicit "deep model compresses, quantum device models the latent" design ([arXiv:1708.09784](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.09784)), followed by the QVAE ([arXiv:1802.05779](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05779)), its end-to-end annealer training ([arXiv:1912.02119](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02119)) and Wilson et al.'s D-Wave-sampled Boltzmann prior replacing uniform GAN noise ([arXiv:1904.10573](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10573)). In the second (2021 to 2025) a trained gate-model circuit supplies the latent: Rudolph et al.'s ion-trap QCBM prior for MNIST ([arXiv:2012.03924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03924)), the trained PQC front-end of QGAN-HG ([arXiv:2101.03438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03438)), Kao et al.'s quantum noise generator, whose latent-supplying variant is the one that improved drug-likeness on QM9 ([arXiv:2210.16823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16823)), the KRAS work ([arXiv:2402.08210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08210)), and Xiao et al. In the third (2023 to 2026) an untrained quantum sampler supplies the latent and the comparison is controlled: ORCA's ICLR 2023 workshop poster ([OpenReview emPnxmK5Jlp](https://openreview.net/forum?id=emPnxmK5Jlp)), Jin and Merz's hardware-noise latents, in which tens of millions of bitstrings from a fixed 16-qubit IBM circuit are mapped through a Gaussian copula and lower FID by up to 17 percent for BigGAN on CIFAR-10 against both Gaussian and simulator-derived priors ([arXiv:2507.01886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01886)), Bacarreza et al., and two continuations on the same ORCA hardware: ensemble GANs for anomaly detection in continuous biomanufacturing, which cites both reference papers ([arXiv:2508.21438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21438); [doi:10.1021/acs.iecr.5c04183](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.5c04183)), and the peptide GAN preprint ([doi:10.64898/2026.07.09.736951](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.09.736951)). No survey published through November 2026 has a category for this line; Tian et al.'s TPAMI survey reaches it only through the QBM-prior QVAE ([arXiv:2206.03066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03066)) and Islam et al.'s 2025 QGAN survey has a "quantum-enhanced classical GAN" bin but does not cite Rudolph or boson sampling ([arXiv:2506.18002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18002)), so the line has to be assembled from primary papers.

Xiao, Zhai, Huang, Fan and Zeng's "Quantum deep generative prior with programmable quantum circuits" appeared in Communications Physics 7, article 276, on 15 August 2024, is open access, has no arXiv version and had 17 Crossref citations at the time of the notes ([doi:10.1038/s42005-024-01765-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-024-01765-9)). A hardware-efficient circuit with angle encoding, CZ entanglers and parameter-shift gradients produces Pauli expectation values that serve as the **120-dimensional latent code of a pretrained 256 x 256 BigGAN**, and the circuit parameters and generator are optimised jointly under a task loss; the classical baseline is the Deep Generative Prior of Pan et al. ([arXiv:2003.13659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13659)), itself a descendant of Deep Image Prior. The experiments are optical ghost-imaging reconstruction with a physics-based forward-model loss at varying sampling rates, including 128 x 128 biological samples outside the pretraining distribution, plus category transfer, super-resolution, colourisation and inpainting measured by PSNR and SSIM, with the claim that QDGP beats DGP "by a significant margin" on inpainting and colourisation and improves out-of-distribution generalisation and diversity. The simulation is the part most relevant to the reader: circuits were run in TensorCircuit with vmap and JIT, either as **18-qubit state-vector sub-circuits each producing part of the latent or as a matrix-product-state circuit with bond dimension 16 emulating a 120-qubit circuit**, the MPS variant used as a diagnostic for barren-plateau risk, with a depolarising channel for noise, on a 192-core Intel server with **two 24 GB RTX 3090s** plus an HPC cluster; the code is public ([GitHub](https://api.github.com/repos/XiaoTailong/QDGP_GI_ImageTasks)). R2 is the only member of the line that uses a pretrained large generator and inference-time optimisation rather than training a generator from scratch, and its motivation comes from the group's earlier ghost-imaging work ([doi:10.1038/s42005-023-01290-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-023-01290-1)).

Bacarreza, Farnsworth, Makarovskiy, Wallner, Hicks, Sempere-Llagostera, Price, Francis-Jones and Clements of ORCA Computing posted "Quantum latent distributions in deep generative models" on 27 August 2025, revised it in January and June 2026, and published it in the Proceedings of the 43rd ICML, PMLR 306, pages 4740 to 4760 ([arXiv:2508.19857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19857); [PMLR v306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/bacarreza26a.html)). Its three contributions are a proof that, under assumptions on the generator, a quantum latent lets a model produce distributions that could not be efficiently produced classically, built from sampling-complexity classes closed under Lipschitz maps and measured in the Wasserstein-1 metric with boson sampling as the quantum task (a lift of the classical latent-complexity argument of Hu et al., [arXiv:2307.08283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08283), onto the hardness template of Coyle et al.); an intuition for when this helps (strongly correlated, multimodal or quantum-process-rooted data); and benchmarks. The experimental design is the paper's real strength: the latent families compared are Gaussian, Bernoulli, "photonic" (the same optical circuit with distinguishable, non-interfering photons) and "quantum" (indistinguishable photons), at latent sizes 16, 32 and 48, on a synthetic boson-sampling dataset (8 photons in 16 modes with Haar-random unitaries and a WGAN-GP-style generator) and on QM9 with Frechet ChemNet Distance plus validity, uniqueness and novelty averaged over 20 seeds. On QM9 with size-32 latents the **ORCA PT-2 (16 photons in 32 channels, 500,000 samples from one loop-based circuit) gives FCD 1.410 plus or minus 0.07, against 1.414 for the simulated quantum latent, 1.560 for distinguishable photons, 1.646 for Bernoulli and 1.823 for Gaussian**. The appendices are as informative as the headline: generating one million size-48 samples with the Clifford and Clifford algorithm took about 3.75 hours on 100 AMD EPYC 7003 processors, which capped the latent size; StyleGAN on CIFAR-10 shows no benefit (inception scores 7.57, 7.66 and 7.54 for Gaussian, Bernoulli and quantum); a different QM9 training regime erases the statistically significant differences, and the authors state the benefit "depends on the interplay between the latent distribution, the model architecture, the training procedure, and the structure of the data"; quantum latents in denoising diffusion GANs and flow matching are shown as compatibility demonstrations rather than performance validations; and classical approximations of boson-sampling statistics are acknowledged as potential alternatives that are "likely to be impractical for machine learning applications". The paper characterises all prior work in the line, including Xiao et al. (its reference 19), as comparing "a trained quantum distribution" against "an untrained classical baseline", which "makes it difficult to draw generalizable conclusions". ORCA's code release reproduces only Figure 1 so far ([GitHub](https://github.com/orcacomputing/quantum-latent-distributions)).

Read together, the two papers differ on exactly the axis R1 identifies as decisive, and the theory section explains why each sits where it does. R2 trains the quantum prior jointly with the generator, so part of its gain may come from having a learnable latent at all; the natural control, a classical learnable latent such as a small normalising flow or a Gaussian with trained mean and covariance optimised under the same DGP objective, is absent from R2 and from its citers. R2's small trained circuit is also the kind of model the dequantisation results bracket as classically surrogatable at small scale (its expectation-value latent rests on the universality result of Barthe et al., which is shared with classical networks), and the closest positive theory for an adversarially or task-trained prior is Letcher et al.'s discriminator result, which assumes a specific discriminator rather than a deep frozen BigGAN between the circuit and the loss. R1 freezes the sampler and controls for everything except interference, which the Herbst anticoncentration result retroactively vindicates: a boson-sampling latent of this kind could not have been trained in the first place. But R1's hardware-scale latents sit in the loss regime where classical imitations with matched low-order statistics exist, and its distinguishable-photon control removes interference without matching the marginals, so its results cannot be attributed to intractability without a spoofer baseline (a Villalonga or Oh-style sample bank, or a classical latent with matched two-point statistics). Kurkin et al.'s finding that basic boson-sampling Born machines are not universal but become so with post-processing is consistent with R1's design, in which the classical generator is a heavyweight learned post-processor ([arXiv:2603.11014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11014)), and the enablers for the obvious next step, a trained photonic latent, now exist: exact parameter-shift gradients for single-photon linear optics ([arXiv:2409.16369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16369)), a trainability framework for photonic circuits ([arXiv:2607.21544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21544)) and Quandela's classical training of photon-native generative models ([arXiv:2603.08793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08793)). The open question that neither paper tests is whether a trained quantum prior beats a trained classical prior of equal cost.

## Realistic directions for a fluids researcher with two RTX 4090s

The practical constraint is simulation capacity, and the arithmetic is simple. A state vector costs 2^n x 8 bytes in complex64, so one 24 GB card holds a **31-qubit forward pass, 30 in complex128, and with adjoint differentiation (ket plus bra-sized buffers) about 28 to 29 trainable qubits**; pooling both cards through CUDA-Q's multi-GPU backend or PennyLane's MPI build adds exactly one qubit, the consumer cards have no NVLink-class fabric so global-qubit gates are PCIe-bound, and 32 qubits in single precision needs 32 GiB and does not fit ([CUDA-Q state-vector simulators](https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/latest/using/backends/sims/svsims.html); [Lightning GPU docs](https://docs.pennylane.ai/projects/lightning/en/stable/lightning_gpu/device.html)). That ceiling is far above anything in the applied literature, where trained generators have 2 to 20 qubits, and it means the two cards are better used as independent workers for seeds and ablations than pooled for one extra qubit. The tooling that fits a PyTorch workflow is PennyLane with the lightning.gpu device (cuStateVec, adjoint on GPU, complex64, batch_obs to cap per-observable buffers) behind TorchLayer for end-to-end gate-model priors, TensorCircuit-NG (the maintained successor of the library R2 used) with its PyTorch or JAX backend for MPS circuits, where **a 120-qubit circuit at bond dimension 16 is about 1 MB** and bond dimension 2048 is about 15 GiB, and TorchQuantum for prototypes below about 20 qubits ([TensorCircuit-NG](https://github.com/tensorcircuit/tensorcircuit-ng); [arXiv:2107.10845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10845)). Photonic latents are a CPU problem: exact boson-sampling samples cost about n 2^n operations each via Clifford and Clifford ([arXiv:1706.01260](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01260)), so a PT-2-sized latent (16 photons in 32 modes) is minutes of CPU per million samples, the 8-in-16 synthetic dataset has only 490,314 output states and can be enumerated exactly with Perceval's SLOS backend ([Perceval backends](https://perceval.quandela.net/docs/v1.3/backends.html)), ORCA's repository depends only on numpy, numba and torch, and R1 itself worked from one million cached samples; both GPUs should go to the classical generator. Avoid new builds on Xanadu's Strawberry Fields, MrMustard and The Walrus (archived in 2026), Qadence (unmaintained) and the stale qulacs-gpu wheel; MerLin provides PyTorch photonic layers if trainable photonic priors are wanted ([arXiv:2602.11092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11092)). The workstation's base Anaconda environment has none of torch, PennyLane or Qiskit, so a dedicated environment is the first step, and GPU work should be scheduled around the fluids jobs that keep the cards busy.

Given that budget and the gaps the notes document, five directions are realistic, and all of them are numerics problems before they are quantum problems. The first and cheapest is the controlled latent-distribution benchmark that neither reference paper ran. Reproduce R1 at latent sizes 16 and 32 (the sizes that need no cluster), then add the missing controls: a classical latent with matched low-order statistics or a spoofer sample bank, a trained classical latent (a small normalising flow or a Gaussian with learned covariance) at the same parameter and sample budget as the quantum one, hyperparameter search on both sides and variance over seeds as Bowles et al. prescribe, and the coverage-style generalisation metrics of Gili et al. that R1 does not report. The same study can carry R2's missing control, a classical learnable latent under the DGP objective, since R2's stack ran on two RTX 3090s. Packaging the latent distribution as a plug-in axis for QUARK's QGAN module or Xanadu's qml-benchmarks generative template would be a small, publishable contribution, because no existing benchmark suite has that axis.

The second direction uses the reader's comparative advantage: physically generated data with ground truth. The three fluids papers all treat the quantum device as a learned distribution sampler, which maps naturally onto stochastic subgrid or particle-closure models. Concretely, the user's SPH, potential-flow and shallow-water solvers can produce vorticity or free-surface fields for the Hsain and Abbou recipe (VQ-VAE latent plus QCBM or QGAN) with a serious baseline in place of their LSTM, Lagrangian velocity-increment PDFs for the Ingelmann et al. recipe at 10 qubits, or inflow statistics for a Q-Prior in the style of Wang et al.; the places where hardware-run quantum samplers have claimed gains are heavy-tailed, multimodal dependence structures (SAR copulas, financial copulas, interference-structured QM9 latents), and turbulent small-scale statistics have exactly those features. A fluid-field dataset with known governing equations also allows the one evaluation the image papers cannot do, checking generated samples against conservation laws and spectra rather than FID on 8 x 8 digits, which several diffusion authors already concede "is a weak metric here" ([arXiv:2609.31070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31070)).

The third direction is end-to-end resource costing, which the literature simply does not do: no diffusion or flow paper reports wall-clock or energy against a classical GPU, cross-platform hardware benchmarks rest on a single run per platform ([arXiv:2405.04401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04401)), and the measurement overhead is folded away. A numerics-style cost model that accounts shots per expectation value (the 128 of the IQM transformer, the 4,096 per basis of Quantum MeanFlow, the 10^4 hardware shots against 10^5 simulator shots of the ibm_brisbane diffusion run), best-of-N post-selection, circuit submission latency and classical pre- and post-processing, applied to the published pipelines, would be a useful paper on its own and a prerequisite for any fluids application. The shot-efficient diffusion result that reaches comparable quality with 303 shots per sample against a 1.6 million-shot reference shows the design space is wide open ([arXiv:2610.41721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41721)). The fourth direction rides the "train classically, deploy quantum" wave with the GPUs doing what they are good at: IQP or spectral Born machines at 100 or more qubits trained on binarised or quantised flow features, evaluated with Raj et al.'s coverage critique rather than converged MMD, with an optional later deployment on IBM hardware of the kind Balló-Gimbernat and Ryu demonstrated. The fifth is theory-adjacent and purely classical: quantify the train-versus-deploy discrepancy of a Pauli-propagation or tensor-network surrogate of a QCBM on fluid data, in the manner of the Born Ultimatum, which is the kind of careful negative result the field now values.

On venue fit, the pattern in the data is explicit: ICML accepted the ORCA paper because it combined a theorem, hardware and controlled baselines; the IQP trainability paper and Wavefunction Flows reached NeurIPS 2026 on theory; and application-driven generative work lands at NeurIPS ML4PS, MLNCP-style workshops, Physics of Fluids, Quantum Machine Intelligence or Machine Learning: Science and Technology. A fluids-data quantum-prior study with the controls above would fit ML4PS or an IOP journal first, and only a result with a provable component or a decisive negative control would fit a main track. Hardware access is not required for any of the five directions; the field's own trajectory (Google's 68 qubits aside) shows that the credible claims are made by the groups whose simulations are careful.

## Conclusion

The decisive shift of 2025 and 2026 is that quantum generative AI stopped being a contest of architectures and became a contest of controls. Every family in the taxonomy now has a representative paper, but the results that survive scrutiny are the ones that fix the quantum component, match the classical baseline and report variance, and those results consistently show modest, regime-dependent gains from quantum samplers inside classical generators rather than quantum generation of classical data. The theory that arrived in the same window turns this from an empirical observation into a structural one: trainability, hardness and dequantisation together leave three narrow routes to advantage, and the quantum-prior design of the ORCA paper is the only one of the three that a researcher without hardware can study with ordinary GPUs, which is why it reached ICML while the quantum diffusion literature stayed in physics journals and workshops.

For the reader this reframes the opportunity. The scarce resources in this field are not qubits but careful experiments and datasets with ground truth, and a fluids researcher owns both: solvers that generate heavy-tailed, multimodal statistics on demand, and a numerics culture that costs every operation. The useful contribution is not another hybrid model but the controlled study, the cost model and the physically checkable benchmark that the two reference papers and their citers leave open, built on two 4090s and a CPU sampler, with hardware deployment as an optional epilogue rather than the premise.
